# 90-Day Lock-In Carousel — Spoken Script (~100 sec, 10 slides)

Structure adapted from a top-performing Instagram video (women-targeted
"great lock-in" program framework), retargeted to men. Every payload number
is cited from the knowledge base.

**Slide 1 — Hook** (`slide1-hook.png`)
> The 90-day lock-in starts now. If you've got the last 12 weeks of the year to build your best physique, you don't need someone's cookie-cutter program — you need the framework to build your own. Five steps, real numbers, under 90 seconds.

**Slide 2 — Step 1: Pick your split by days** (`slide2-split.png`)
> Step one: your split is a schedule decision, not a personality test. Three days a week? Full body. Four days? Upper/lower — the proven sweet spot. Five or six? Push/pull/legs. Pick the one you'll actually show up for — consistency beats optimization every single time.

**Slide 3 — Screenshot this: upper/lower split** (`slide3-ul-split.png`)
> Okay, you're going to want to screenshot the next two slides. This is the entire four-day upper/lower split, and every line is one of the movement slots I'll show you in a second. Monday, upper one — you're pushing first: horizontal press, vertical press, horizontal pull, then isolations and core. Tuesday, lower one is hamstring-focused: hinge first, squats after. Thursday, upper two flips the order — vertical pull, horizontal pull, then vertical press. Friday, lower two is quad-focused, and there's no hinge that day — just squats, isolations, and core. Pick any exercise you love to fill each slot.

**Slide 4 — Screenshot this: full-body split** (`slide4-fb-split.png`)
> Only got three days? Screenshot this one instead. Monday: squat, horizontal press, horizontal pull, plus isolations and core. Wednesday: hinge, vertical press, vertical pull, plus isolations and core. Friday: the same slots as Monday, filled with new lifts. Full body three days a week is the simplest version of this that works — every muscle, every session.

**Slide 5 — Screenshot this: your 90-day plan** (`slide5-plan.png`)
> So you picked your split. Now you need the three-month plan — this is month one: three sets of six to ten on compounds, two sets of ten to fifteen on isolations, three to four reps from failure. Month two, add a set to everything. Month three, same sets, but one to two reps from failure. Every fifth week, deload — half the sets, same weights.

**Slide 6 — Step 2: Your exact numbers** (`slide6-numbers.png`)
> Step two — here are your exact numbers, no math required. Every compound: three sets of six to ten reps, resting two to three minutes — four to five on heavy leg work. Every isolation and core move: three sets of ten to fifteen reps, one to two minutes rest. Effort: stop one to three reps shy of failure and write every set down. And cap it at about six hard sets per muscle per session — past that, you're just collecting fatigue. Do that two to three times a week per muscle and you're automatically in the ten-to-twenty-set growth band.

**Slide 7 — Step 3: Fill slots, not exercises** (`slide7-slots.png`)
> Step three: build each session from slots — a squat, a hinge, a horizontal press, a vertical press, a horizontal pull, a vertical pull, core, then isolations for what you want to grow. Compounds in the 6–10 range, isolations 10–15, and pick any exercise you like for each slot.

**Slide 8 — Step 4: The engine** (`slide8-engine.png`)
> Step four, the engine that drives all of it: every week, do a little more than last week. Bench press, three sets of six to ten — week one, six, six, six. Week two, seven, seven, six. Keep adding a rep until you hit tens across, then add a little weight and start over. Write down every set — notes app, notebook, whatever. That's how you know it's working.

**Slide 9 — Step 5: When to change exercises** (`slide9-swap.png`)
> Step five — and this is where everyone messes up: don't swap exercises every month. If your numbers are still climbing, change nothing. Swap only when a lift stalls for a full block. Novelty feels productive; progression is productive.

The complete 90-day program — every split, every session, every set already laid out. This is the exact framework from the carousel, filled in for you. Pick your track, follow the monthly plan, beat last week's numbers. That's the whole job.

01How the 90 days work

Three months, three jobs. Each month gets slightly harder — more sets, then closer to failure — while the exercises never change. You add reps every week; when you hit the top of a rep range on every set, add a little weight and start the climb again.

The weekly rule: beat last week's reps on every set. Bench 3×6–10 — week 1 you get 6, 6, 6. Week 2, 7, 7, 6. When you hit 10, 10, 10, add a little weight and start over at 6s. Write every set down (notes app, notebook, whatever) — that's how you know it's working.

05The rules that make it work

When to change exercises: you mostly don't

Don't swap exercises every month. If your numbers are still climbing, change nothing. Swap only when a lift has been stuck for 4–6 weeks — same reps, same weight, no progress. Novelty feels productive; progression is productive.

Deload weeks (5 and 10)

Half the sets, same weights, everything feels easy. You're letting your body catch up, not pushing for records — in week 6, go right back to the weights you were using in week 4.

If a whole week goes bad

Missed sleep, work blew up, or two rough sessions in a row — just repeat the previous week instead of pushing forward. Never add extra exercises to "make up" for missed sessions.

Want more of a specific muscle?

From month 2 onward, you may swap one exercise per session for a different one that hits the muscle you care about most (e.g., incline press in place of shoulder press on Upper 2 for more chest). One swap — not a rebuild. The volume table below shows where you have room.

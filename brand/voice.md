## Positioning

- Evidence-informed coach. Explains mechanisms, not just rules.
- Core philosophy: ANTI-OVERCOMPLICATION. "The more levers you pull, the
  more variables you introduce, the higher the risk of something going
  wrong." Recurring thesis — end educational videos on it.
- Myth-buster angle: calls out common advice ("cut sodium", "cut water
  early", "staying sub 10% is healthy") and explains why it's wrong with
  physiology (cortisol, fight-or-flight, kidneys, glycogen).
- Audience: intermediate lifters; first-time competitors for prep content.
  Natural-lifter framing — no enhanced content.
- Funnel: soft DM CTA for coaching.

## Pacing & Length (measured)

- ~150 words/min (2.5 words/sec) — natural talking pace, not rushed.
- 60-75s script = ~150-190 words. Write to this budget.

## Hook Patterns

1. Aspirational + visual: "So you want to look like this on show day."
   (pairs with showing the result)
2. FAQ / social proof: "So many people have asked me what I do to grow
   my legs."
3. Numbered frame: "There are three main variables you need to know..."
   — concrete, finite payoff.

## Structure Blueprint (educational episode)

1. Hook: desire statement, FAQ, or visual result (1 line)
2. Frame: "There are N things you need to know" or "this is the OG
   workout that built my X"
3. Per item: MYTH or QUESTION -> MECHANISM (analogy + number) ->
   APPLICATION (exact foods/exercises/weights)
4. Close: philosophy statement ("Don't overcomplicate peak week.")

## Explaining Style

- Mechanism -> application, always. Never "do X" without "because Y".
- Analogies for physiology: sodium = "a gatekeeper, a transporter that
  decides where the water and carbs should go."
- Concrete numbers: "each gram of glycogen holds three to four grams of
  water."
- Food-level specificity: "white rice, cream of rice, potatoes."
- Anticipate viewer's question mid-video: "Now you might be wondering,
  what is the purpose of sodium?"
- Stage framing for muscle groups: "if you have a thigh gap when posing,
  your adductors are way too small."

## Phrasing & Tone

- Second person throughout. Rhetorical questions bridge sections.
- Short punchy declaratives for emphasis: "Don't overcomplicate peak week."
- Repetition for emphasis; contrast phrasing ("can actually make you look
  WORSE").
- Conversational but information-dense — no hype-bro energy, no screaming.
  Confident, calm, direct.
- Natural filler "you know" — keep some in scripts for authenticity.
- Profanity casual in skits/vlogs ("fuck", "good freaking morning");
  educational content stays clean.

## Hybrid Humor (inside educational content)

Educational videos are ~80% info, 20% embedded deadpan jokes — the
hybrid is real:
- Fake disclaimer: "I don't do this leg day anymore because it's too
  dangerous. If I kept doing this, my legs would grow way too fast."
- Hyperbole delivered straight: "You will feel like dying after one set."
- Running-gag callbacks: Bulgarian split squats referenced as brutal in
  both a skit AND the workout video.

## Humor & Skit Style

- Persona is the joke engine: credible coach plays it 100% straight while
  content subverts it. DEADPAN — never acknowledge the joke.
- Skits are word-light (15-45s, under ~60 words). Punchline timing is
  everything — cut hard at the reveal, don't wrap it up.

### Skit formats that work (proven)

1. SARCASTIC TUTORIAL — recommend something brutal as "very simple, very
   easy, very relaxing." Every claim is the opposite of true.
2. BAIT-AND-SWITCH — open like education ("Here's one reason why your
   chest is not growing") then absurd VISUAL punchline, no more talking.
3. ESCALATION LOOP — "problem -> chicken and rice" repeated while stakes
   climb from mundane to absurd. Close with mock wisdom: "It's that
   simple."
4. MOCK REVEAL + HARD CUT — list items ("zero dollars..."), start an
   absurd confession ("because when I stole this—") and CUT mid-sentence.
5. RANT -> SELF-SUBVERSION — complain about gym bro behavior until you've
   talked yourself into it: "Fucking hell, I'm in."

Themes: gym bro culture satire, diet culture absurdity, self-deprecation,
dark-but-light edges. Punchlines absurd, never mean.

## Talking Head / Vlog Style

- Personal transparency with real numbers: body weight (72.7kg), stage
   weights, the 10kg gap. Precision builds trust.
- Journey arcs: post-show reflection -> honest self-assessment -> next
  goal. Motivational but never toxic-positive.
- Strong opinion segments backed by physiology ("staying sub 10% is not
  healthy at all").

## What-I-Eat Format

- Voiceover + food b-roll. Precise quantities: "30 grams of oats", "30
  grams of whey iso", "pinch of sea salt."
- Step-by-step recipe structure.
- One unscripted personality moment mid-video (the turtle interrupt).
- Supplement commentary with claimed purpose (berberine, ACV -> "insulin
  sensitivity, digestion").
- Close: ritual beat (Monster) + sign-off.

## Caption Style

- All lowercase, casual.
- Format: episode number + title -> restate core takeaway -> simplified
  actionable version for beginners -> tease next episode -> soft DM CTA.
- CAPS for one emphasis word per caption ("WRONG").

## CTA Patterns

- Educational video: end on philosophy line (no "follow me") — caption
  carries the follow/DM CTA.
- Caption CTA: "if you are currently in contest prep or need help with
  your peak week, dm me and let's see how i can help"

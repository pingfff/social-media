# Fixed 4-Day Upper-Focus Split — Ping (v2)

**Lifter:** [programs/profiles/Ping.md](profiles/Ping.md) · **Goal:** hypertrophy (chest & back priority)
**Split:** Upper-Focus Hybrid (3 upper + 1 lower) · **Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri, fixed weekdays** · **Target session length: ≤75 min**

**Design logic in one paragraph:** v2 of today's design, re-run after an intake refresh — training age, goal, priorities, equipment, and constraints are unchanged, so the specialization structure carries over (legs cut to one low-volume maintenance day, volume reallocated to chest/back at 2–3 exposures/week [knowledge/07 specialization branch, T17, T18]). What changed: the schedule is now **fixed weekdays** instead of rolling, so the week locks to the synchronous 4-day layout (Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri) that the decision tree prescribes for fixed schedules [07, T17], and the deload is now **by feel** rather than scheduled (profile Q19: "by feel"). Volume allocation, exercise selection, and progression are identical to v1.

## 5. Block structure & progression

**Block = 4 overload weeks** [knowledge/04, T6] — no calendar-scheduled deload week in v2 (see §6). Method: **double progression** (rep accumulation → set addition), matched to an intermediate training age [T1, T2].

- **Week 1 (calibration):** start every exercise at RIR 2–3 with ~2 reps left in the tank on paper — deliberately light to leave progression room. Log every set.
- **Weeks 2–4:** add 1–2 reps per set weekly at the same effort. When **all** sets of an exercise hit the top of its rep range, add a set the next week (caps: 5 sets/exercise [T6]; chest ≤18/wk, back ≤20/wk).
- **Exercise-swap trigger:** if an exercise's reps fall below ~6 across a block, swap it for a same-muscle variation [T6].
- **Between blocks:** something must change, even small — rotate which press angle leads (incline-first ↔ flat-first [chest/1]), or restart the block one rep-range notch harder via deeper stretch/ROM as the "load" increase [T1 — technique as an overload route].
- **Stalling 2 weeks on an exercise:** hold volume there and let the logbook decide (below).

## 6. Deload plan (by feel)

**Changed from v1:** your refreshed profile selects **by-feel deloads** (Q19: b) instead of a scheduled week — so no Week-5 deload is placed on the calendar. When a deload fires, it's the same **volume deload** as v1 — this is a volume-driven hypertrophy program, so cut volume, keep movement quality [T15]:

- Halve all working sets (3 → 1–2, 2 → 1); drop the optional slots entirely.
- Same exercises, same rep ranges, everything stopped at **RIR 3–4 — nothing to failure anywhere**.
- Sessions should feel easy. "If your light week still feels hard, you timed it right" [T15].

**Triggers that fire a deload week** (knowledge/06): light weights feel heavy, can't get a pump, soreness lingering past ~2 days, motivation to train drops, joints complain, or Monday's numbers drop two sessions running. You train habitually near failure at 4×/week — accumulated stress *will* eventually fire one of these [T15, T6]; the by-feel choice just means you respond to signals instead of the calendar.

## 7. Autoregulation & adjustment triggers

- **Logbook rule [T18]:** if the next session's numbers drop → the previous session's volume was too high → cut 1–2 sets from that session and rerun. If numbers rise, you found your sweet spot — iterate, don't inflate.
- **Dial back when** [T15, T20]: the §6 trigger list — any two of them in the same week means take the deload now rather than "pushing through one more week."
- **Lower-back beat up:** swap U2's rack pull/SLDL → chest-supported/seated back work, or skip axial pulls that week (localized deload [T15]).
- **Elbow irritation:** switch skull-crusher-style movements out for cable variations, keep tension through the pinky side of the wrist [arms/2]; change exercises rather than push through [arms/3].
- **Missed a day (fixed schedule):** don't double up — pick the session back up next week in its slot; priority muscles keep frequency as long as Mon/Thu/Fri survive intact. If a full week collapses, drop U3 for that week and resume Monday [T10 — a program that can't flex fractures].

## 8. Design rationale & contradictions

**Branch taken (knowledge/07):** Not a novice → no full-body default. No current program to audit (Step 0 n/a — flagged in Assumptions). **Fixed-weekday branch:** schedule requires fixed weekdays → synchronous 4-day Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri layout [07; T17 U/L template]. **Specialization branch** layered on top: one strong area (legs) cut to a maintenance day, volume reallocated to priority muscles (chest/back) trained 2–3×/wk [T17, T18]. Split container is the J3U upper/lower session template adapted to 3 uppers: push-biased and back-biased upper days with chest-braced back work to manage fatigue carryover [T17]; the week's only floor pull sits Thursday, fresh after Wednesday rest, with no hinge-heavy session following it (the classic collision 07/03 warn about [T17]).

**Volume allocation:** priorities at 14–16 sets — mid-band of 10–20 [T1, T6], capped well under the back >25 risk line [back/1], spread so no session exceeds ~6–7 hard sets per muscle region (session-quality plateau, [T4, T22]). Arms at the arms/1 baseline rather than specialization numbers. Legs at retention-plus per your explicit request — a legitimate maintenance call under the volume-landmark framework [T22], not an oversight.

**Contradictions touched (knowledge/08 §Known Contradictions) — positions taken:**

1. **Failure training (#1) / RIR (#11):** T21's always-failure vs 2–3 RIR baseline. **Position:** hybrid per the converging machine-safety theme — free-weight compounds stay 1–3 RIR, machine/cable/isolation last sets may go to failure [T3, chest/1, back/2]. Context: your logged habit is 0–3 RIR, so this matches how you actually train without license to grind heavy DB presses to failure.
2. **Bro split (#2):** rejected for priority muscles — chest/back frequency 2–3×/wk (T18/T21 advanced-position + your "enjoy hitting muscles often"), consistent with the 7-day-gap plateau failure mode.
3. **Frequency (#3):** T22's low-volume-high-frequency vs volume-equated consensus. **Position:** moderate volume at 2–3×/wk distribution — the distribution-quality argument [T18]; full T22-style high frequency not adopted because your recovery budget is self-rated average.
4. **Volume norms (#4):** T6's higher numbers vs T22's diminishing ROI >10–12. **Position:** priorities sit at 14–16, justified because sets are distributed across sessions and you're intermediate with an average recovery budget — not elite volume.
5. **Per-session ceiling (#6):** T22 ~6/muscle vs T23 15–16. **Position:** ~6–7 per muscle region per session, with U2's 10 back sets split across three regions (lats/upper back/floor pull) as in the pro pull-day template [back/6].
6. **Deload necessity (#7):** **changed in v2** — by-feel deloads adopted per your explicit preference, replacing v1's scheduled 4–6-week volume deload. The scheduled position's context (near-failure habits at 4×/wk accumulate stress) is respected by making the §6 trigger list concrete and binding: any two warning signs = deload that week [T15, T6 vs T16].
7. **Arm volume (#8):** arms/1 baseline (3–6 direct sets) chosen over arms/3 specialization — arms are not a stated priority; indirect work from 16 back sets + pressing is substantial.
8. **Rear delts (#9):** overrated-if-back-training-is-good [shoulders/2] vs start-every-session [shoulders/3,4,6]. **Position:** minimal direct (2 sets/wk) because back volume is high and varied — the "proper back training" context Israetel conditions on. Reassess visually after a block.
9. **Calves (#10):** dedicated protocol [legs/1] vs life-load theory [legs/3]. **Position:** optional 2 sets — you asked for low leg volume and they're a non-goal; the legs/3 position fits a low-priority calf case.
10. **Lengthened bias (#12):** stretch-first picks throughout (incline cable fly, Bayesian curl, overhead extension, behind-body laterals) per [T3, chest/2], tempered by back/2's finding that trained lifters grow fine on full ROM — full ROM is the non-negotiable floor, stretch-bias is the tiebreaker between otherwise equal exercises.

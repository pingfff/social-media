# Program Design — Evidence Summary

## Split selection

- Adherence + recovery beat optimization. PPL/Upper-Lower/Full Body/
  Arnold — all work when weekly volume per muscle is equated.
- The best split is the one you'll actually train hard on and recover
  from. Intermediate default: Upper/Lower 4x or PPL 5-6x.

## Exercise order

- Compounds first (heavier, more systemic fatigue), isolation later.
- Weak point exception: priority muscle can be trained FIRST on its day.

## Mesocycle structure

- Accumulation 4-8 weeks -> deload.
- Week 1 near MEV, add sets and/or reps weekly (double progression),
  approach MRV by final week, deload, repeat with slightly higher
  starting volume or load.

## Deloads

- Every 4-8 weeks, OR when: reps dropping across sessions, joint aches,
  sleep/resting heart rate worsen, dread of training.
- Prescription: cut sets roughly in HALF, keep load the same or slightly
  lower. Keep intensity, remove fatigue.

## Intensity techniques

- Drop sets, rest-pause, myo-reps: similar hypertrophy to straight sets,
  major time savings. Best on isolation work late in a session.
- Do not replace heavy compound straight sets with them.

## Autoregulation

- Rep targets + RIR caps. If you miss rep targets two sessions in a row
  (with food/sleep normal), deload early.
- "The program is a hypothesis; the logbook is the data."

## Program hopping

- Switch when: stalled after a deload + nutrition/sleep checked, or
  every 2-3 mesocycles for variety. Switching weekly = no accumulated
  progress.

## Weak points

- Add sets to the priority muscle by SUBTRACTING from a strong muscle.
  2-3x frequency, stretched-position exercises, train it first.

## Script angles

- "The best split (serious answer)"
- "5 signs you need a deload (and exactly how to take one)"
- "Stop program hopping — here's when to actually switch"
- "Bring up a weak bodypart without junk volume"

# Hypertrophy Training — Evidence Summary

## Volume

- ~10-20 hard sets per muscle per week is the productive range for most.
- Landmarks (Renaissance Periodization model): MEV (minimum effective) ->
  MAV (adaptive) -> MRV (maximum recoverable). Varies by muscle AND person.
- Mesocycle structure: start at MEV, add sets weekly, deload before/at MRV.
- More volume = more growth UNTIL recovery becomes the limiter. Junk
  volume past MRV = regression.

## Frequency

- 2x+ per muscle per week beats 1x at equal total volume.

## Proximity to failure

- 0-4 RIR works; 1-3 RIR is the sweet spot for most sets.
- Isolation/light exercises: go closer to failure.
- "Light weights build muscle IF you actually take them near failure" —
  most people's "3 RIR" is actually 6-8 (RIR prediction error, SBS 2024).

## Rep ranges and rest

- ~5-30 reps to failure all stimulate growth (Schoenfeld 2017).
  Pragmatic: 5-10 compounds, 8-15 isolation.
- Rest: 2-3+ min compounds, 1.5-2 min isolation. Short rest kills
  volume-load.

## Progressive overload

- Double progression: hit top of rep range on all sets, then add load.
- Logbook beats feelings. Track reps + load + RIR.
- Overload = stimulus, not just poundage: adding weight while shortening
  range of motion or losing control = fake progress.

## Range of motion / stretch

- Full ROM default. Emphasize stretched position: incline curls, overhead
  extensions, RDLs, deep squats, deficits.
- Lengthened partials: emerging evidence (Wolf 2024 meta) — partials in
  the lengthened position comparable to or beating full ROM at long
  muscle lengths. Frame as "new frontier", not settled law.

## Script angles

- "Sets per week: the only chart you need"
- "Your '3 reps in reserve' is actually 7 (and it's costing you gains)"
- "The logbook beats feelings — track these 3 numbers"
- "Why light weights DO build muscle (with one catch)"

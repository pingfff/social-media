# Peak Week: Training, Cardio, Sleep & Travel

*Source: Peak Week transcripts 1–19 (PW#), websites 1–2 (W#). See `../08-source-map.md` for the index.*

## Principles

### Training
- The hard work is done before peak week; training matters less than nutrition but is easy to muck up [PW13]. Athletes commonly **overtrain** in peak week out of motivation/fear [PW6].
- The stimulus only maintains tissue — "the fatigue is not worth it at this point" [PW6].
- **No soreness allowed** — muscle damage inhibits glycogen uptake ("if you're sore Friday trying to fill your chest out, tough") [PW8].
- Legs are trained **early in the week** so leg definition isn't blurred by inflammation on show day [PW1] [PW13].
- Night-before pump training must not be hard enough to damage/inflame (inflammation retains water and blurs lines); a good pump confirms you're hydrated and not over-depleted [PW2].
- Guru protocols that stop all training entirely are part of the criticized package [PW12]; inflammation from a huge leg day the day before the show is pointless and water-retaining [PW13].
- Light training keeps digestion and nutrient shuttling moving [PW9]; "just stimulate the muscle so you're not burning muscle" [PW4].

### Cardio & steps
- Convert formal cardio into steps; maintain condition on the *least* cardio possible [PW6].
- Taper cardio through the week so recovery and hormonal processes ramp up while stress drops [PW9].
- Standardize a **low step count** through the week — beware the silent expenditure drop from walking around at 2-days-out to sitting in a hotel room at 1-day-out [PW3].
- Don't stress about incidental walking during the carb load — activity is minimal relative to prep cardio [PW8].

### Sleep & recovery
- Poor sleep raises cortisol → morning water retention [PW6].
- Life/interpersonal stress counts as a fatigue driver ("being a little mean to your girlfriend") [PW6].
- Sleep is always better than trying to force food on the day of a contest [PW19].
- If the client can take peak week off work to walk outside, that's lower-fatigue than treadmill output [PW6].
- Make peak week "a little bit selfish" — traveling with a crowd catering to family adds stress [PW6].

### Monitoring & coaching
- Judge changes from **consistent documentation**: same lighting, same time, same poses, plus logged meals/fluids/weights in a running chart [PW3].
- Photos 3×/day: morning (tightest but flat), midday (≈ the ideal show-day look), night (fullest, less tight); use the pattern to decide carb increases [PW14]. Rarely change anything off a wake-up photo — wait for post-first-meal photos [PW16].
- Vascularity is a gauge of hydration adequacy and of approaching the spill line — vascular while flat means no spillover yet; if vascularity is "hiding," check room temperature and possibly raise water with the carbs [PW14].
- Self-assessment on contest day is "forensically diagnostically difficult" — a coach's objective eye beats your mirror; keep detailed year-to-year notes because physiology differs contest to contest [PW18].
- The best competitor behavior: proactive communication with full context [PW19].
- Off-plan secrets are dangerous: a client who privately took a diuretic and didn't tell the coach ("I could have killed you because that's really important for me to know") [PW19].

### Posing
- Posing is the one "training" that continues [W1].
- On stage, 15–30 min of flexing plus sweating crushes fullness — another reason to keep intramuscular water high [PW16].

- **Cardio exceptions**: weight-class athletes may need set output kept; some small individuals simply need more cardio — "maintenance on the least cardio possible within reason" [PW6].

### Making weight (weight-class athletes)

| Rule | Detail | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Full rest one day out | Trust instincts — no forced 12k steps or training; guaranteed fatigue drop and lighter weigh-in | [PW6] |
| Low cardio + low food > high cardio + high food | Fatigue holds weight; food volume in the gut holds weight; rest, some steps, lower food volume (hungry, but lighter on the scale) | [PW6] |

### Hunger management (PW4 n=1 strategy)

| Tactic | Detail | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Ramped water + constant walking | Suppressed hunger almost completely | [PW4] |
| Morning walk timing | Hunger disappears 20–30 min into a morning walk while drinking water | [PW4] |
| Stay busy/hydrated | — | [PW4] |

- Deficit context from PW4: >2 lb/week for 5.5 weeks; mid-week blood test testosterone 478 vs normal low-500s, still feeling good; mindset shift credited [PW4].

### Sleep protocol
- One night out: use every previously-tried sleep agent at its max working dose (melatonin, herbal formulas); Jewett also uses a histamine blocker (Benadryl) for sleep, doubling as allergy cover while traveling [PW6]; Feather: "take a Benadryl and go to sleep" [PW8].
- Oversleeping on show day is fine [PW19].

### Travel & routine protocols

| Rule | Detail | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Flight timing | Mid-morning flights arriving mid-afternoon; travel day is an off day (2 a.m. wake-ups wreck eating/fluid windows and sleep) | [PW6] |
| Keep routine identical | Wake-time changes and late fluid/food explain mystery "soft" mornings; standardize electrolytes, condiments, fluids | [PW6] |
| Off days around travel | — | [PW6] |
| Arrive early to rest | Rico traveled to the show hotel one day early to rest | [PW1] |

### Posing protocols
- 15 min Tue/Wed/Thu and 15–20 min Friday in the [W1] plan; Friday = posing only [PW8] [W1].
- Two-day shows drain fullness through prolonged pumping/posing; small sodium + water increases restored it dramatically [PW18].

## Contradictions & Context

- Cardio amount: "least cardio possible" [PW6] vs PW4's up-to-11-mile walking days and weight-class athletes keeping set output — context (deficit size, body size, making weight) explains the difference (see 08-source-map).
- Training detail: Feather's Wed/Thu pump-style glycogen-shuttling training [PW8] vs Jewett's pure volume deload [PW6] — both agree on no failure/no soreness; disagree on whether training aids filling.
- Sleep agents: Benadryl use [PW6] [PW8] is a coach anecdote, not a health recommendation — general-fitness framing applies.

## Gaps

- No source quantifies how much cardio reduction changes cortisol/water retention (effect size unknown; mechanism only).
- No data on sleep-agent safety in very-low-body-fat, low-food states.
- Whether light pump training actually improves glycogen storage vs simply avoiding damage is asserted [PW8] [PW13], not tested.
- Travel direction/time-zone effects (east vs west, long-haul) not covered.
- No coverage of female-specific cycle-timing interactions with peak-week cardio/sleep scheduling beyond the one mention in file 01.

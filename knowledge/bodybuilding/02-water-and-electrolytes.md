# Peak Week: Water, Sodium & Potassium

*Source: Peak Week transcripts 1–19 (PW#), websites 1–2 (W#). See `../08-source-map.md` for the index.*

## Principles

### Fluid compartments (why manipulation protocols fail)

- Body water is ~2/3 intracellular, 1/3 extracellular [PW7] (cited elsewhere as ~63–65% ICF / 35–37% ECF, of which ~28% interstitial and 7–9% other [W1]; ~70/30 [PW12]).
- Compartments adjust in a **one-to-one ratio**: if extracellular water drops, intracellular drops too — cutting water makes you proportionally flatter, not "drier-skinned" [PW7] [PW12].
- You cannot selectively drop subcutaneous/interstitial water while keeping intramuscular water — "near impossible," not supported by current science [PW7] [W1]. Interstitial fluid is negligible and tightly regulated [W1].
- The extracellular space is also the **vascular space**: dehydrating dehydrates veins — no vascularity, concave arm veins, no pump [PW7].
- Outside of sweating, dehydration lowers blood volume and blood pressure uniformly — hence poor show-day pumps [PW11].

### Aldosterone / RAAS

- The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system reacts **within an hour** of a water shift; aldosterone peaks ~24–40 h after — i.e., the rebound from a Thursday manipulation lands exactly on show day [PW7] [PW12].
- Army sodium-restriction study (retold by Norton) — full baselines: blood sodium 139 mEq/L (normal ~140), urinary sodium 217 mEq/day, aldosterone 10.4 ng/100 mL. After 6 days of restriction blood sodium dropped only ~1 mEq/L, urinary sodium fell to ~1/10th, and aldosterone **doubled in 2 days, tripled in 3** — the body conserves sodium and reabsorbs water [PW12].
- Sodium is typically cut Thursday (2 days out), so aldosterone is doubled/tripled exactly on stage day [PW12].
- Aldosterone-driven reabsorption plus lowered vascular pressure leeches water into the interstitial layer: "you've put water exactly where you don't want it" [PW12].

### Water follows solutes

- Drinking a lot of water does not make you hold water — output scales with intake; excess is urinated out [PW7] [W1] [PW15].
- "You literally wake up every morning dehydrated. That's just a physiological fact" — everything drunk the prior day is urinated out overnight; you can't pre-load tomorrow's hydration [PW19].
- "You cannot over hydrate if you don't have too much carbohydrate — that water will flow through you" [PW15].
- Cottonmouth during a big carb load even while drinking a gallon: carbs pull that much fluid into tissue — normal, don't chug in response [PW8].
- After a sharp water drop-off, re-adding water before the diuretic response finishes can leave you watery — the response has to play itself out [PW8].
- Why mild dehydration can still "work" for the very lean: little fat-cell water remains, so the small detail gain is worth the small fullness loss [PW7] — "a little bit goes a long way especially if you're stage lean already" [PW7].
- "Hydrated equals striated" — you need hydration for fullness (muscle pushing against skin) and visible detail [PW7] [PW14].

### Na/K pump and solute transport

- The Na/K pump moves 2 K in per 3 Na out — potassium can't enter the cell without sodium; cutting sodium stalls the pump, potassium piles up outside cells and draws water there = spillover [W1].
- "The transport system for carbohydrates is sodium" (Revelia) — carbs can't reach muscle cells without it [PW11].
- Low sodium during a carb up downregulates the (sodium-dependent) intestinal glucose transporter → less glucose absorbed, more solute drawing water into the gut → bloat and GI distress [PW12].
- Sodium raises blood pressure acutely → better pumps; have it before training and before the stage [PW13] [W1].
- Backstage high-sodium food mechanism: rapid sodium entry into the bloodstream gives a vascularity effect, pulls water into the vascular space (may slightly dry the interstitial space) while drawing fluid into muscle cells — total-body hydration is still required [PW18].
- Supportive minerals: calcium and magnesium support the Na/K pump; usually adequate from food, but audit clients eating narrow diets; canned tuna/chicken can make sodium extraordinarily high already; whole-food eaters rarely need added potassium [PW18].

Pre-peak hydration tracking (Klemczewski/Atkinson): 1–2 weeks out, note how day-to-day look swings (softer/fuller/tighter/vascular) correlate with hydration — builds the reference range for contest-day interpretation [PW19]. If full but not pumping, the fix may be dissipating with heat or light activity rather than more inputs [PW19].

Case studies of under-drinking: a bikini/figure competitor who refused show-day water ("I was still hydrated from earlier in the week") came out with arms/shoulders "literally half the size," losing three close classes [PW19]; a male client 15–20% flat where "16 more ounces while pumping up would have put you over the edge" [PW19].

### Show-day sodium use

- Sodium is the main show-day lever once glycogen is stored: "more carbs the day of the show isn't going to help, but just a little bit of sodium really pulls all that water in" [PW15].
- Most important sodium window is **30–60 min pre-stage** [PW18]; a sodium load ~30 min before stage [PW16]; ~1 tsp salt with caffeine 15–20 min before pump-up acutely raises BP for a pump [PW13].
- "If you can't get a pump, it's almost 1,000% a sodium issue" — pinch more salt; pump usually returns within 20 min [PW19].
- Water before food in the morning (food slows water uptake), with a couple pinches of salt as a small front-load [PW16].
- Keep show-day seasoning identical to the rest of the week — competitors silently stop salting "because it's show day"; prescribe condiments explicitly; Atkinson adds Morton Lite Salt on top so sodium never drops on show day [PW16].
- Sodium pre/during pump-up is "a rescue mission" to hold glycogen during long waits [PW16]; high-sodium foods backstage (pizza, a Subway sub) give rapid vascular entry — see mechanism under Principles [PW18].
- Sodium retention/excretion timing: uptake starts in minutes, full effect takes hours, and ~2 days after consumption about half is still in the body — plan accumulation across two-day shows [PW18].
- Over-shooting is possible: re-dosing every 2–3 h can "spill over from sodium" — a fine line [PW18]; don't change sodium upon waking for an afternoon stage (escalates too early); ramp small doses toward stage time instead [PW18].
- Sodium audit example: a client on 2× electrolyte packets (2,000 mg each) plus food hit 7–8 g/day; reset weeks out — "sodium is very adaptive… less is more; when we add a little bit when we need it strategically it'll have a better effect" [PW18].
- Glute-fullness example: sodium added ~2 h out (Ava Cowen) produced the best glutes of her career [PW12].

### Potassium rules

- Loading potassium while cutting sodium is "stupid and useless": a too-high K:Na ratio retains water in the kidney's distal tubule, and excess potassium can hyperpolarize cardiac cells and stop the heart (lethal injection = potassium chloride) [PW12].
- Chugging potassium tablets while cutting water "can cause a heart attack" [PW15]; avoid 99 mg potassium tablets — an unnecessary variable affecting kidneys and aldosterone [PW18].
- Supportive ratio ~3–4:1 sodium to potassium (why sports drinks list sodium first) [PW18]; Jacked Factory guideline 4–5:1 added Na:K [W1].
- Keep normal fruit/vegetable potassium sources; don't need to track it if food sources are rich [PW15] [PW18]; whole-food eaters rarely need added potassium [PW18].
- Morton Lite Salt (50% Na / 50% K) is Atkinson's go-to — safer than tablets, guarantees both minerals; Klemczewski wrote about it ~15 years earlier but found users salted so sparingly they under-consumed sodium [PW15].
- Pre-stage dosing method: mix a mineral packet into a gallon, split into 12–16 small bottles so each bottle is a known dose you can schedule [PW18].

### W1 sample plan (water/sodium/potassium detail)

- Mon: 10–12 salt shakes per meal + 2 gal water; vegetables only asparagus/spinach; fats as avocado/nut butter [W1].
- Tue–Fri: water 1.5 gal; Tue carbs only post-workout + last meal; no water 60 min before the carb meal and only ~6–8 oz after [W1].
- Fri: only 4–6 salt shakes with the evening + prior meal [W1].
- 100 mg potassium supplements with specified meals [W1] — flag: the potassium tablets contradict PW15/PW18 (see Contradictions #18).

### Health warnings

- Water + sodium depletion together is the classic dangerous combination [W2] [PW7].
- Dehydration + diuretics: backstage collapses, medic calls, a Texas woman's stroke, one host's renal failure from a full Dyazide tab with no fluids [PW5] [PW12] (details in `06`).

# Peak Week: Principles & Prerequisites

*Source: Peak Week transcripts 1–19 (PW#), websites 1–2 (W#). See `../08-source-map.md` for the index.*

## Principles

### What peak week can and cannot do
- Peak week is not magic: it won't add muscle or make you shredded; it mostly "enforces what you've already done" [PW13]. At best it makes an already-great physique ~1–5% better [PW13] [PW8].
- You can't do much to look drastically better, but you can easily do things that make you look worse [PW13].
- Peak week optimizes "the little things"; done wrong it can lose all the hard work of the prep [PW1].
- "Peak week is not magic… based on data collection… not rolling the dice on show day" — J3U frames the week as finding, through daily measurement, the food/fluid amount that produces the best look, then mirroring it on show day [PW3].
- "Most competitors look better the week before or the week after the show" — the cost of chasing magic peak-week formulas [PW2].
- "I looked great a week out but looked like crap on show day — couldn't get a pump, wasn't full" is the classic guru-protocol outcome; nobody ever says "I looked like crap a week out but peak week fixed everything" [PW12].
- Norton: not a fan of "sacrificing 50% to try to get 5%"; prefers someone 95% ready 100% of the time; adjustments should be subtle and within what's normal for that person's body [PW12].
- Only three primary variables matter: carbohydrates, water, and sodium/potassium balance [W1].

### Stage leanness is the prerequisite
- "You have absolutely no place starting a peak week once you are not done and got rid of all the body fat" — fat pockets absorb the extra water retention and look washed out [PW1].
- Peak week cannot fix body fat: "you can do all the manipulations you want with water and sodium, those things don't magically rid your body of fat" [PW2].
- "You cannot peak the fat away" — even a few pounds off condition can obscure everything peak week does [W2].
- Meadows targets being in shape **2 weeks out**, with all separation/detail showing [PW2].
- "If you are not shredded heading into peak week it's not going to matter what you do" [PW12]; "when you're absolutely shredded peaking is actually not that difficult" [PW11].
- If still behind at peak week, better to keep getting aggressive on fat loss than to peak-manipulate [PW2]; if not stage-lean a week out, use the week to diet or pick a later show [W1].
- Benchmark for stage lean: study photos of people winning the shows you want to enter; truly stage lean means feeling terrible, flat workouts, moody, low blood pressure and heart rate [PW11].
- "I was holding water on stage" is usually a euphemism — unless you had edema in hands/sock lines around the ankles, you simply weren't lean enough [PW12].
- Once very lean, water retention itself largely resolves: with little body fat there is little adipose water left to hold [PW6].
- Ideally the athlete is "peaked almost before peak week" — arriving with the look already there [PW6], completely shredded the week before [PW9], ideally after a diet break with multi-day refeeds so carb response is predictable [PW9] [W2].

### Fatigue, not water, is the usual culprit
- **Fatigue is the biggest cause of peak-week water retention**: low body fat, low food, hard training, cardio, steps, poor sleep, and life stress all raise cortisol; cortisol acts on the mineralocorticoid receptor (same receptor as aldosterone), driving water retention [PW6].
- "Dropping fatigue is going to mean dropping water" — Jewett has peaked clients simply by tapering cardio and training; no food/water manipulation needed [PW6].
- Large peak-week weight drops are usually **not new fat loss** — they're fatigue dropping, water flushing, and exposing fat loss that was masked by cortisol-driven retention all along [PW6].
- Other retention culprits: GI upset from new foods, menstrual-cycle timing in females, routine changes (earlier waking, late food/fluid), electrolyte/condiment variation, PEDs [PW6].
- "You are not smarter than your body… fluid dynamics are regulated on a minute-to-minute basis" [PW12].

### Predictability
- W2's final principle: prioritize **predictability** — the more variables changed simultaneously, the less predictable the outcome and the harder it is to attribute cause; change only what's necessary [W2].
- If still losing fat entering peak week (i.e., not reverse-dieted), more aggressive initial carb increases may be warranted [W2].
- Enhanced competitors' peaking processes are already in motion and "have to play themselves out" — stick to the plan, no last-minute changes; naturals can assess and adjust morning-of [PW8].

### Core physiological facts (referenced across files)
- Each gram of glycogen binds ~3 g water [PW4]; ranges cited: 2–4 g [PW13], 3–4 g [PW7] [W1] [PW8]; the 2.7 g figure traces to old rat-liver studies [PW8] [W1].
- Muscle is ~70–75% water [PW13] [PW14] [PW18]; "a flat muscle is a soft muscle, a full muscle is a hard muscle" [PW2].
- Total-body glycogen capacity ≈ 23 g/kg lean tissue (~2,300 g for a 100 kg male); a 180-lb human stores ~350–500 g muscle + 60–120 g liver glycogen; bodybuilders can exceed 800 g [PW8] [W1].
- Fullness = carbohydrates + water + sodium; water follows solutes (glucose, sodium, potassium) [PW7] [PW19] [W1].
- Carb assimilation into glycogen takes 24–48 h — nothing eaten on show day finishes filling you that day [W2] [PW13] [PW18].
- After a long prep the body is exceptionally insulin sensitive and preferentially stores glucose as glycogen [W1].

### What peak week is not for
- Not for losing more body fat [W1].
- Not for shedding water weight — reducing total body water is not an objective [W1].
- Not for "thinning the skin" — the fish-and-asparagus skin-thinning idea has no physiological basis; thinner skin just means leaner [W1].

## Protocols & Numbers

### Strategy-selection factors

| Factor | Question / rule | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Which show matters | Cliff Wilson explicitly asked which show mattered most and how judges weigh leanness vs fullness vs mass — different answers dictate different peaks (full vs diced-but-full) | [PW1] |
| Pose-by-pose evaluation | "Who wins the pose as a whole" — sometimes accept a slightly flat body part to keep condition where it matters (e.g. letting glute striations fade for a fuller chest/back spread) | [PW3] |
| Stage timing | Best look in the evening → load heavy ~2 days out; best look fasted in the morning → hold it; best look 2–3 h after eating → repeat the found meal pattern before an early-afternoon call | [PW3] |
| Practice peaks | Run a mock peak on yourself ~3–4 weeks out (e.g., the last deload) to learn your response; peak weeks only get complex if you have a decision tree from a mock peak | [PW8] [PW5] |
| Athlete conditioning | For less-conditioned athletes (first show, warm-up show): "less is almost more in every circumstance" — keep carbs AND water consistent since new vascularity appears daily anyway | [PW8] |
| Fat-loss status | Still losing fat entering peak week (not reverse-dieted) → more aggressive initial carb increases may be warranted | [W2] |

### Division differences (leanness gradient)

| Division / level | What "in shape" means | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Figure / bikini / fit model | Appropriate fullness differs by division; over-hardness is penalized in bikini ("runaway metabolism" clients may need fats as a brake) | [PW17] |
| Women's physique vs figure vs high-level male bodybuilding | What "in shape" means differs by division; higher levels require striations and "deep separation… very very hard to get" [PW2] | [PW2] [PW17] |

### Natural vs enhanced

| School | Position | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Naturals (mainstream) | No aggressive water/sodium manipulation — naturals lack the compounds that cause extra water storage; pulling water/sodium just makes them look worse; cutting water/sodium 1–2 days out as a natural is "a very good way of making you look worse" | [PW8] [PW13] |
| Natural BB history | Natural bodybuilding has moved away from water/sodium manipulation (credit: Klemczewski) — 2016–17 British natural BB questionnaire study (reviewed by MASS): most competitors still manipulated carbs/sodium/sometimes potassium; water manipulation has decreased over recent decades in natural BB | [PW11] |
| NPC/IFBB | NPC/IFBB coaches still widely cut water and sodium — Revelia disputes the "nobody manipulates water anymore" claim for NPC/IFBB from firsthand client plans | [PW11] |
| Naturals can still be aggressive | Aggression of peak is coach-dependent, not federation-dependent: a natural (Rico, coached by Cliff Wilson) still ran a 1,000 g rapid carb backload under daily check-ins | [PW1] |
| GDA warning | Jared Feather warns naturals off glucose-disposal-agent supplements (berberine etc.): poorly studied, potentially liver-dangerous, some may secretly contain metformin-like compounds | [PW8] |

### Evidence the peak, not the athlete, was the problem
- Revelia clients who were never successful at Nationals won pro cards after he replaced their water/sodium-cutting plans — evidence the peak, not the athlete, was the problem [PW11].

### The traditional/guru protocol, and why it fails

| Protocol | Description | Outcome / risk | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic guru protocol | Early-week depletion workouts, load sodium/water and cut carbs; late-week cut water/sodium, load carbs/potassium, add a diuretic, stop training | Flatness misread as "too few carbs" → more carbs → overload → spillover; competitors often look best a day or two *after* the show once they eat sodium-rich food freely | [PW12] [W2] |
| Old-school severe protocol | Pull water, pull sodium, load potassium, fish-and-asparagus only | "You look terrible on show day," feel worse as the show approaches; on a very lean athlete it can be **dangerous** | [PW9] |
| Conventional peaking verdict | — | High risk, low predictability, dangerous, now disfavored by most coaches | [W2] |

- The post-show "cheat meal made me look fantastic" phenomenon is not carb magic — the meal restored sodium and water freedom, letting the athlete finally fill out [PW12].

## Contradictions & Context

- Natural vs NPC/IFBB water manipulation: Revelia agrees water manipulation has declined in *natural* BB (crediting Klemczewski) but disputes the same for NPC/IFBB based on firsthand client plans — see 08-source-map § Known Contradictions (water/sodium manipulation).
- Coach-dependent aggression: a natural athlete running a 1,000 g rapid backload [PW1] vs naturals needing no manipulation at all [PW8] — peak aggression tracks coach philosophy, not federation (see 08-source-map).
- "Most competitors look better the week before or after" [PW2] vs guru-protocol claims of show-day magic — see 08-source-map (conventional vs evidence-based peaking).

## Gaps

- No source gives a division-by-division quantitative leanness benchmark (e.g., body-fat % targets per division); only qualitative descriptions of the gradient.
- No data on how common mock/practice peaks are among coached competitors, or measured error rates of first-time vs practiced peaks.
- The British natural BB questionnaire study is cited secondhand (via MASS review) — effect sizes and methodology not covered in the transcripts.
- No source covers how often judges explicitly penalize over-hardness vs under-conditioning per division.
- Stage-timing heuristics [PW3] are given as patterns, not as tested protocols with outcomes.

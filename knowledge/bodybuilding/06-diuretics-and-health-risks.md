# Peak Week: Diuretics, Health Risks & Horror Stories

*Source: Peak Week transcripts 1–19 (PW#), websites 1–2 (W#). See `../08-source-map.md` for the index.*

## Principles

### Consensus: no source endorses routine diuretic use
- Meadows doesn't use diuretics with anyone anymore — "too much risk… you can't accurately predict where the water's gonna come from"; even potassium-sparing agents (e.g., Dyazide) leave people peeing and flat [PW2].
- Norton has never had a client take a diuretic; his figure clients were told by judges they looked "too hard" — fullness + hardness came from hydration alone [PW12].
- "Diuretics just cause a snowball": they drive sodium loss (water follows), and the worst move is cutting sodium and water at the same time → massive dehydration [PW7].
- Jewett: diuretics may have some application but usually miss the mark; diazide stays in the system ~8 h — you go flat, eat carbs, nothing happens, and it's too late to fix [PW7].
- Diuretics "are not dangerous if you know what they're doing — what they strip, what they spare, their half-life and active duration"; what goes wrong is the pulling of water [PW5].
- Diuretics should not be the first response to holding water — address root causes (fatigue, cortisol, sodium consistency) [PW6].

### Coach accountability
- Coaches must own their decisions whether or not they pan out [PW5].
- Honesty about diuretic use is a safety issue given the salts coaches prescribe [PW19].

### Electrolyte and over-pumping mechanisms
- Excess potassium can hyperpolarize cardiac cells and stop the heart (lethal injection = potassium chloride) [PW12]; potassium tablets while cutting water "can cause a heart attack" [PW15].
- Raising water without sodium risks hyponatremia — if water goes up, sodium must go up a little [PW8].
- Water + sodium depletion together (conventional peaking) is "extremely dangerous" [W2]; at no point should you feel dehydrated during peak week [W1].
- On a very low-body-fat athlete, the old severe protocol (pull water/sodium, load potassium, fish-and-asparagus) can be dangerous, not just ineffective [PW9].
- Rapid backload's timed potassium-then-sodium protocol carries health risks if mistimed [W2].
- GDAs/supplements marketed for carb-shuttling may be mislabeled or secretly metformin-contaminated — "good luck to your natty status" [PW8].
- Over-pumping mechanism nuance: as pumped-up glycogen burns, water is released with it — flatter AND looking spilled [PW16].
- Backstage panic pattern: a coach flattens the athlete then "just put in some gummy bears backstage… it never works" — glycogen is partly fluid; you must have water for carbohydrates [PW5].

### Framing
- Water/sodium manipulation and diuretics "are not just irrelevant. They are absolutely harmful to your look on contest day. Many of them harmful and risky to your health" [PW18].
- The body's homeostatic survival mechanisms (fluid balance keeps the heart beating and brain perfused) fight any massive manipulation within hours [PW18] [PW7].
- Bro myths persist because a manipulated look occasionally lands — "many competitors get advice from anonymous bodybuilding gurus" [W1].
- Sauna analogy: aggressive manipulation might look better briefly, but backstage you stand for hours with unknown stage timing — you need to look great *all day*, which only consistency delivers [PW11].

## Contradictions & Context

- Diuretics "not dangerous if you know what they're doing" [PW5] vs "no source endorses routine use" consensus [PW2] [PW12] — PW5's pharmacology framing is conditional expertise, not endorsement; context is the distinction between knowledge and application (see 08-source-map).
- Jewett allows "some application" for diuretics [PW7] while Norton has never used one with a client [PW12] — coach-philosophy difference, not a factual dispute.
- General-fitness framing: this file is content-source material about competitive-BB risks, not medical advice.

## Gaps

- No source provides a pharmacokinetic table (half-lives, active durations) for the specific agents named (Lasix, Dyazide, HCTZ) beyond diazide's ~8 h.
- No quantified incidence data for diuretic-related hospitalizations in bodybuilding (cases are anecdotes).
- Long-term health outcomes after renal-failure-type events (recovery, kidney function) not covered in any transcript.
- Whether the "small amount between prejudging and finals" use [PW5] has ever been documented to work safely is not covered.
- Women-specific diuretic/electrolyte risk interactions not discussed.

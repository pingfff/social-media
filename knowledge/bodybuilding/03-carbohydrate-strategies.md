# Peak Week: Carbohydrate Manipulation Strategies

*Source: Peak Week transcripts 1–19 (PW#), websites 1–2 (W#). See `../08-source-map.md` for the index.*

## Principles

- Glycogen binds water: the traditional rationale for depletion + reload is the ~1 g carb : ~3 g water binding ratio [PW4]. Water follows solutes: carbs and sodium rising together pull water into the muscle [PW9].
- Glycogen storage capacity is finite: Feather pegs it at ~23 g/kg lean tissue — though he has personally exceeded this multiple times (see Protocols) [PW8].
- Assimilation takes time: you can't fully assimilate glycogen on contest day anyway (a 24–48 h process) [PW18] [PW19] [W2]; carbs take time to digest, so adding 600–1,000 kcal in 24 h works only for specific athletes [PW9].
- Post-prep insulin sensitivity cuts both ways: long dieting leaves the athlete depleted and insulin sensitive, so a sudden huge load risks spillover; depletion also suppresses metabolism at the worst time, and with ~48 h to the contest supercompensation "starts to bleed out anyway — you only get the bad" [PW18]. Conversely, a big refeed after long dieting can ramp the metabolism so it burns through the load [PW9].
- **Spillover** = more carbs than can be stored as glycogen; excess glucose spills into interstitial space with water following → watery, bloated, smooth [W1]; or glycogen/water leaking outside the muscle creating softness between skin and muscle [PW9]. Under conventional water/sodium cutting, high extracellular glucose draws the limited water away from muscle and just under the skin [W2].
- Spillover nuance: the problem is excess glucose itself, not water; de novo lipogenesis is inefficient so an overshoot doesn't become fat — it sits interstitial pulling water [W1]. Spills are usually a sodium/water problem, not a carbs problem — don't train hard to "burn off" a spill [PW8].
- Dose–response extremes: too small a load = flat; correct amount = muscles "popping" with vascularity [W1]; over-carbing while dehydrated is the worst outcome — flat AND spilling [PW15]. When unsure, "better a little flat than spilling over" [W1].
- Spill shows first in the midsection — watch it for getting washed out [PW3].
- Peaking has three goals, and one is keeping the **waist** sharp — don't overdo carbs the day before/of the show so the waist stays small for posing [PW8].
- Over-carbing on show day: "you keep shoving carbs in, all you do is get softer and you spill over because you can't assimilate that" [PW16].
- Clean-eating competitors can underestimate sodium needs; eliminating all sodium makes you "crash hard into losing all your muscle fullness" [PW17].

### Front Load — detail

- Protocol (Revelia, from Layne Norton's approach): deplete Sat/Sun before, very high carb Monday, taper to the Saturday show, small Friday bump [PW11] [PW9].
- Worked early (Revelia won his first 3 shows against dehydrated NPC competitors) [PW11].
- Flaws: best look arrives Tuesday/Wednesday and fades toward the show; the big Monday refeed ramps a suppressed metabolism so weight (and fullness) drops through the week; the Friday re-feed never matches the early-week look [PW11] [PW9].
- Front load deliberately overshoots to find the spillover threshold, then cleans up over 3–4 days — the adjustment window is its main advantage [W2]. Favored by bikini/figure/wellness [W2]. W2 detail: front load holds protein/fat steady, slightly reduced at peak carbs [W2].

### Mid Load — detail

- Low carbs early, gradually increasing to a peak 2–3 d out; the small expected "spill" on the last load day is corrected in the final 1–2 days; protein/fat slightly reduced on highest-carb days [W2]. Popular with bikini, figure, wellness, Men's Physique [W2].

### Back Load & Rapid Back Load — detail

- Back load: 3–4 d very low carb (protein/fat high), then a rapid carb increase the final 2–3 d targeting supercompensation up to show day; like conventional but **without** water/sodium depletion [W2]. W2: back load + clean-up used in men's BB/classic and women's BB/physique [W2].
- Rapid back load (Cliff Wilson): 1–2 d modest carb increase → 4–5 d depletion at near-normal calories (deplete carbs, not calories; protein/fat high then significantly reduced) → >800 g carb load the day before the show, with timed potassium then sodium loading in the final 24 h [W2].
- Rico's rapid backload under Cliff Wilson (natural, WNBF/INBA): pre-peaked by "eating into the show" (carbs raised from a prep low of 130 g to 220 g/day); ~350–400 g carbs Sat–Sun; <100 g/day Mon–Thu at 1,884 kcal with protein/fat raised; Friday = **4,546 kcal, ~1,000 g carbs across 12 meals every 1.5 h** (rice cakes, raisins, lean steak, chicken, sea-salt potatoes, dextrose/maltodextrin mix, an isotonic drink mix (cyclist-type powder), a glucose-disposal agent for final meals); expected swing: below 70 kg depleted → ~70.5–71 kg pumped [PW1]. Sat–Sun high-carb days totaled 2,645 kcal vs 1,884 kcal Mon–Thu [PW1]. Backload protein was kept low on purpose — "not trying to trigger protein synthesis," only forcing carbs into muscle cells [PW1]. Rico entered peak week 3 kg heavier than his previous show [PW1].
- Everything changed on daily check-ins (photos, weight, data); Wilson judged Rico's metabolism fast-adapting from a practice 510 g day the prior Sunday — he could have given more carbs but stayed cautious [PW1].
- Risks: timing-critical (fill too slowly = flat; too fast = spill), demands an already completely shredded physique and an expert coach [PW11]; core bloating and spillover even with a clean-up day [W2]; adding 600–1,000 kcal in 24 h works only for specific athletes — carbs take time to digest [PW9].
- PW4's traditional variant (self-coached): keto Mon–Wed, Thursday keto + sodium cut, Friday carb up on "dry carbs with almost no water"; observed 3–6 lb swings in 3 days from carb cutting + flushing; keto-day calories 2,000–2,400 with protein ~200 g [PW4].
- PW13's natural backload for a two-day show: prep used 3 high days (~500 g) / 4 low days weekly so his carb response was known; Mon–Wed at 200–250 g carbs / ~250 g protein / ~50 g fat; Thu–Sun ~500 g carbs / 200–225 g protein / ~50 g fat; little personal spillover risk [PW13]. A side benefit of the Mon–Wed low days is possibly getting a little leaner before the load [PW13].

### Progressive Linear Load (the modern default) — detail

- Klemczewski's balloon analogy: front load inflates then deflates all week; back load tries to re-inflate at the last minute; linear load starts moderate Monday and adds **every single day** [PW11] [PW18].
- Revelia's numbers: start ~275 g carbs Monday, add 40–50 g/day, adjusted daily on look (spilling? harder? vascularity? fullness?) — "usually the highest carb day of prep the day before the show" [PW11] [PW9].
- Prerequisite: completely shredded the week before, ideally with prior diet break/refeeds; with a big refeed after long dieting the metabolism "kicks on" and burns through the load — hence linear beats a single Friday refeed, which left Revelia hitting a prep-low weight on show-day morning [PW9]. Linear loading has produced athletes dropping weight while adding calories and looking harder daily (metabolism ramping) [PW9].
- Water follows solutes: carbs and sodium rise together, water follows into the muscle — better than guessing carbs/sodium/water in a 24 h window [PW9].
- [W2] variant: competitor already stage lean and reverse-dieting so glycogen is near-full; peak week may begin with only a +15–25 g carb increase; final days add slightly more carbs/fats plus increased sodium; show-day focus is satiety, sodium, water — not carbs. If the competitor is still losing fat entering peak week, initial increases should be more aggressive than +15–25 g [W2].
- Klemczewski case: ectomorphic client dieted at 200–250 g carbs, built through a metabolic phase to 400–500 g, won a pro title; on contest day they just stayed even and tuned with hydration/sodium [PW18].

### J3U meal-by-meal model (one day out / show day)

- One-day-out totals: 240 g protein, 800 g carbs, 100 g fat; weight trajectory 236.2 fasted → 238.8 pre-meal-3 → 241.6 bedtime (~5 lb range) [PW3].
- Show-day mapping example: wake 220 fasted → full/hard 224 by pre-meal 3 → deliberately slightly spilled by meal 5 (since 5–6 lb drops overnight) → on stage 224, then reduce food/fluid only to HOLD that look [PW3].
- If the best look is in the evening, load heavy **two days out** so the remaining days are just top-off [PW3]. Days 2–3 out are mostly about dropping fatigue — by 1 day out the plan is set and predictive [PW3].
- Post-meal tuning method: at a good look on 120 g carbs/12 g fat, add +20 g carbs/+6 g fat, then +30 g/+5 g; when scale weight jumps a lot, repeat the previous meal instead of escalating; fats added alongside so carb volume (waist) stays manageable [PW3].
- Practice peaks during prep; mid-week manipulations should be small (e.g., slightly more carbs on load day 2), not cup-of-rice changes every two hours [PW8].

### Depletion: For and Against

- **Against hard depletion (Meadows):** after a long diet you're already depleted ("you've been depleting for three, six, eight weeks"); super-compensation theory "works for long distance runners, doesn't mean it works for bodybuilders" [PW2].
- Big/muscular competitors have trouble getting OUT of depletion — "it takes days… it's not a matter of eating 2,000 calories the day before"; Meadows credits his success with bigger athletes to not depleting them; his own 212-era hard depletions (especially water) rarely recovered overnight [PW2].
- **Pro depletion (Feather, for naturals):** some early-week depletion then a **two-day carb up**, dosed 10–15 g/kg lean tissue on day 1 (e.g., 15 → 13 → 10); capacity ~23 g/kg lean tissue; 4-day 1,000 g/day loads are "kind of silly" unless training hard [PW8]. Feather has exceeded the ~23 g/kg capacity multiple times — e.g., 1,500 g + 1,500 g + 500 g day-of at a nighttime Nationals with water/sodium tapered (getting progressively drier/cottonmouth) [PW8].
- Feather uses minimal (not trace) carbs on depletion days, deliberately not entering keto [PW8]. If a natural is already shredded at ~100 g carbs, keep them there — the uptake machinery is primed; dropping to 20 g is silly; avoid keto transitions (keto→carb has many individual mishaps) [PW8].
- Reason Rico/PW4 deplete: high-rep glucose-burning training plus <100 g carbs to empty glycogen before the backload [PW1]; traditional rationale is the 1 g carb : ~3 g water binding [PW4].

### Protein & Fat During the Load

| Item | Handling | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | Stays stable unless pulled down ~25% on carb-load days to make caloric room (hypercaloric days carry no lean-tissue-loss risk); comes down to normal or slightly below (~≤1 g/lb) on load days; stick to prep protein sources; accommodate placebo bloating complaints to keep the competitor calm; may be nudged up at the end of a linear load when monitoring supports it | [PW8] [PW17] |
| Backload protein (rapid) | Kept deliberately low — only forcing carbs into muscle cells, "not trying to trigger protein synthesis" | [PW1] |
| Fat | Not "loaded" — "there's no such thing as fat loading"; may rise slightly to chase carbs up calorically and for digestion support | [PW17] |
| Fat mechanism | Almost no thermic effect and doesn't assist the glycogen load; its only real benefit is **glycogen sparing** — a small fat increase at week's end stops carbs being churned through as quickly; "heavy in molecular structure," digests slowly — raise only a little at a time | [PW17] |
| Fat & digestion | Extra fat calories create room to insert a digestive aid so the client keeps going to the bathroom through the load | [PW17] |
| Runaway-metabolism brake | For clients (especially bikini) getting too hard/tight past division criteria as carbs climb, bump end-of-week fats to blunt the leptin spike | [PW17] |
| Fat load to maintenance | Feather's optional strategy for the already-shredded: raise fats so depletion days sit at maintenance (training deloads, activity down — a deficit risks muscle loss); sometimes skipped to keep a deficit for last-bit fat loss in very sharp athletes | [PW8] |
| Fat ramp (intramuscular triglycerides) | Optionally an intramuscular triglyceride strategy for the completely shredded — cites Dr. Scott Stevenson's peaking paper (Schoenfeld/Aragon foreword, Chris Barakat co-author; free online; also a Revive Stronger podcast; Eric Helms wrote an earlier one) | [PW8] |

### Food Choices, Fiber, Volume

- Eat the **exact same foods as all prep** — "we want boring, we want predictable"; unknown foods introduce digestion variables you can't troubleshoot [PW11] [PW13] [PW9]; Rico switched from flexible dieting to a first-time written meal plan of "very safe foods" [PW1].
- Pick foods for **digestibility** and condense portions as small as possible to control the waist during the load [PW3].
- Taper fiber down through the week (e.g., skip the big evening vegetable meal) to avoid GI distress/"food baby" [PW13]; but don't eliminate fiber to the point of harming motility [PW18].
- Keep food **volume** low on high-carb days to avoid a distended stomach [PW13].
- Quantity is the key to the carb load, not food choice — no food is uniquely "best" [W1].
- Sample traditional plan ([W1]): Mon no starch, all asparagus/spinach; Tue carb meals around training (12 oz raw yam etc.); Wed–Thu no starch; Fri evening nibbled meal + a slice of cheesecake; show-day breakfast of eggs/chicken/sweet potato/half slice cake/honey-rice cakes; Gatorade sips if flat.

### Supplements & Aids

- Creatine: keep taking it during peak week — the water it holds is intramuscular, "a different type" from subcutaneous [PW4]. (Contradiction: Rico's coach removes it [PW1].)
- GDAs (glucose disposal agents): used by Rico's coach in the final backload meals [PW1]; Revelia uses "Core Load"/"Core Hard" [PW9]; Feather warns they're poorly studied, possibly dangerous, sometimes secretly metformin-contaminated [PW8]; Klemczewski says "weird supplements" neither push nor shunt carbs [PW8].
- Supplements otherwise should barely change from the rest of prep [W1].

## Gaps

- No source quantifies how much carbohydrate is assimilated per hour during a load (only that it's a 24–48 h process [PW18] [PW19] [W2]); the 12-meals-every-1.5-h structure [PW1] is empirical, not derived.

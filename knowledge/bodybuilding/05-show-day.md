# Peak Week: Show Day, Pump-Up & Post-Show

*Source: Peak Week transcripts 1–19 (PW#), websites 1–2 (W#). See `../08-source-map.md` for the index.*

## Principles

### Show-day philosophy

- Little can be changed on show day — you're **maintaining** a look, not building one; glycogen storage takes 24+ h so stage-day carbs can't rush fullness [PW13] [PW18] [W2].
- Show-day levers are **sodium and water**, not carbs: waking flat is "just sodium and water" — first meal + 16–20 oz water restores veins/vascularity [PW9]; over-pushing carbs just makes you softer and spills [PW16].
- Keep show-day food normal/conservative (the full carb base went in Thu–Fri); keeping food lighter means you're slightly supercompensating from the prior day's stored glycogen [PW16] [PW19].
- Undereating on show day = burning muscle glycogen, urinating out more water than you take in, going flat; overeating = solute pulls water out of cells = spillover — "sometimes just morbidly soft" an hour after looking super tight [PW19].
- Nerves/panic release fight-or-flight glucose that gets burned; a meal can't replace that glycogen in time — lean on sodium and water [PW19].
- Know your stage time and plan backwards [PW3]. Water timed to show start or ~1 h into it, with a "cut off" checkpoint as an extra check-in [PW19].
- At the water cutoff, do a **test pump** (not the real one): could you get a pump? Flat? Full but not vascular? Is the venue cold? [PW19].
- First thing: water before food (food slows water uptake), a couple pinches of salt in it [PW16].
- Morning walk or light training only if you reliably look better post-training [PW9].
- Don't do so much activity (pumping, check-ins, walking) that you deplete glycogen and flatten as the day goes on [PW15].
- Two-day shows: keep sodium/water doses small and repeated; accumulation across days causes its own problems [PW18].
- If full but not pumping, the fix may be **heat/light activity to dissipate** the fullness rather than more inputs [PW19].

### Hydration tracking and water method

- Pre-peak hydration tracking 1–2 weeks out: correlate day-to-day look swings with hydration to build the contest-day reference range [PW19].
- Atkinson's water method evolution: ounces-per-meal intake failed (clients skipped intake around makeup/hair) → cumulative checkpoint targets instead [PW19].

### Pump-up (backstage)

- **Over-pumping is the major mistake**: pumping 35 min burns glycogen; as glycogen burns, water is released — you get flatter and look spilled while the correctly-pumped athlete keeps filling on stage [PW8] [PW16].
- Timing: start when the class **before** yours goes on stage — push-ups, sip Gatorade (fast GI carb); in 10–15 min you're veinier, fuller, hold poses better, and keep filling while others fade [PW8]; start ~15–20 min out; too early = flatten, rushed = poor pump [PW13].
- Pump everything **except legs** — pumping legs stores blood/fluid there and hides cuts/separations [PW13].
- No pump, no fat gain: "if you can't get a pump, it's almost 1,000% a sodium issue" — more salt, pump back within ~20 min [PW19]; a flat pump fix is often just salt + water [PW19].
- If you can't surge to fullness or see no vascularity while pumping, you're still underhydrated (if spilling, it's the carbs instead) [PW19].
- If a whole-food meal was eaten ~2 h before warming up, skip the pre-pump snack — piling on raises spillover risk; use a hydration-based supplement (pre-workout with NO/creatine) plus the sodium-potassium mix instead [PW18].
- A good pump requires hydration: "you can't pump up a dehydrated muscle… a muscle is 50–60% water" [PW2].

### Staying warm & presentation

- Competitors get cold quickly in air-conditioned venues; use "Hot Stuff"/deep-heat to stay warm, even a hair dryer; once the top tan coat is on you can't put a jumper on; don't sweat so much you flatten [PW13].

### Assessment heuristics (coach eye)

- Everyone wakes the tightest of the day ("flat, but tight") but you can't replicate that on stage — you're not horizontal asleep for 8 h pre-stage [PW19].
- Photo test for skeptical clients: one set dehydrated vs one hydrated at noon; the coach picks the hydrated one (only visible if lean enough) [PW19].
- "Butt hang" (saggy, unfilled glutes — Ava Cowen/Brooke Erickson term) comes from low water/low sodium; hydrated muscle is hard, full, separated; depleted tissue is soft and spongy [PW12].
- Being **~5 lb heavier with minimal loss of condition** is usually the ideal stage look [PW3]; chest/back fill most with food; glute striations fade when very full — accept the trade if the pose wins [PW3].
- Concave arm veins = very dehydrated [PW7].

### Post-show & rebound

- Anti-binge framing: the cheat-meal fixation mimics eating-disorder behavior; if hunger dominated the prep, the prep plan needs reassessing [PW8].
- Cereal is fine post-training (high GI) but eating it throughout prep drives excess hunger — cheat-meal-fixated diets need reassessing (RP FPRH reference) [PW8].
- Return to **maintenance** rather than binge-rebounding or crawling up via traditional reverse dieting (staying very lean too long prevents regaining lost lean tissue); a 150 lb man's maintenance ≈ 300–350 g carbs [PW8].
- Mental frame: "the diet lasts one week longer than the show" [PW8].

## Contradictions & Context

- Judge-language caveat: telling competitors to look "dry" implies not drinking water; a first-time judge telling an athlete "you were holding water" is unfounded — the language feeds the myths [PW19]. (Related heuristic: concave arm veins = very dehydrated [PW7]; first-time judges claiming you "hold water" can't actually know that [PW19].)
- Safety-messaging backfire: "when people hear the word safety, they think this is an extreme sport, I don't want to be safe" [PW19].
- Fullness trade-off: being ~5 lb heavier with minimal condition loss is usually ideal [PW3], but glute striations fade when very full — accept the trade if the pose wins [PW3].
- Water method evolution (Atkinson): per-meal ounces prescribed to schedule failed in practice (makeup/hair disruptions) and was replaced by cumulative checkpoint targets [PW19] — illustrates why prescriptive timelines need redundancy.
- Over-pumping vs under-pumping: over-pumping burns glycogen and flattens/spills you [PW8] [PW16], while no pump is usually a sodium problem fixed in ~20 min [PW19] — the error asymmetry favors under-pumping and fixing with salt/water.

## Peak Week / Show-Day Mistakes Checklist

- Starting the peak still carrying body fat [PW1] [PW2] [W1]
- Cutting sodium + cutting water (+ diuretic) simultaneously → flat, soft, unpumpable [PW2] [PW5] [W2]
- Hard depletion when already depleted; big muscles can't refill overnight [PW2]
- Inconsistent daily water making Friday changes unpredictable [PW2]
- Shocking the body with sudden 2-gallon water loads instead of ramping [PW4]
- New foods / diet variety during peak week — unpredictable digestion [PW1] [PW11] [PW13]
- Uncontrolled photo conditions (lighting/pose) when judging the look [PW3]
- Silently dropping activity (hotel sitting) 1 day out [PW3]
- Chasing a diuretic with a gallon of water instead of replacing electrolytes [PW5]
- Over-pumping backstage [PW8] [PW13] [PW16]
- Shoving carbs in all day "because I'm flat" → spillover [PW16] [W2]
- Stopping salt on show day out of fear [PW16]
- Skipping water on show day [PW19]

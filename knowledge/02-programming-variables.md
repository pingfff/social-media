# Programming Variables: Volume, Sets, Reps, Rest, Frequency, Intensity

*Source: transcripts 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 18, 22, 23*

## Volume (sets per muscle per week)

**Baseline recommendations:**
- **10–20 hard sets per muscle per week** for most people/larger muscles [T1, T6]
- 20–30 sets/week claimed for smaller muscles (biceps/triceps) [T6] — set by trial and error
- Worked example from T6: chest 16, back 14, hams/glutes 10, quads 12, biceps 26, triceps 22 sets/week

**Volume landmarks (weekly net stimulus framework, T22):**
- **~3 sets/week per muscle = retention/maintenance**
- **6–8 sets/week once-weekly = net growth surplus**
- **>10–12 sets/week = diminishing ROI** ("minuscule" gains relative to fatigue cost)
- Within a session, stimulus plateaus around ~6 hard sets per muscle

**Per-exercise/per-session caps:**
- Max ~5 sets per exercise per session; need more volume → add another exercise (different angle) [T6]
- ~6 sets per body part per session before diminishing returns [T4, T22]
- Session volume decays in quality — first sets are highest quality [T18]

**Volume preferences from practice:**
- 2 sets near failure × 3 different exercises/angles > 4 sets of one exercise (people sandbag when more sets are coming) [T4]
- Don't count ramp-up/rack-run warm-ups as work sets [T4]
- Elite bodybuilders historically ran 20–50 sets/workout — effective for gifted/chemically-assisted athletes, not norms for naturals [T2, T20, T22]
- Cutting volume below your current level can become an "atrophy program" — always check your current per-muscle volume before switching splits [T17]

## Rep Ranges

- **6–30 reps all produce equal hypertrophy when taken near failure** (meta-analysis) [T6]; even 5 to 30 [T4]; up to ~30 [T1]
- Below ~6: works but repeated heavy loading raises injury risk — not recommended as default [T6]
- Practical pairing: **compounds → lower reps (6–10); isolations → higher reps (10–15, sometimes 12+)** [T6, T8]
- Choose reps per exercise by joint tolerance & suitability: squats feel good 6–10 (12+ limited by breathing, not legs); curls 12+ (under 10 irritates elbows) [T6]
- Slight rep-range undulation across the week adds novelty [T6]
- Time-restricted training: 8–12 reps (~3 s/rep) is the time-efficient sweet spot; 15 OK for calves/arms [T23]

## Rest Periods

- **Minimum 1–2 minutes between sets; 2–3 min is the hypertrophy baseline. 3 min > 1 min for hypertrophy** (Schoenfeld study) [T3, T4, T23]
- Heavy legs may need **4–5 min** [T4]
- Short-rest training (CrossFit-style) is a poor growth stimulus [T4]
- Smaller muscles (biceps) need less rest — less lactate/H+ accumulation [T23]
- Rest capacity is **trainable**: one study showed a group that reduced rest from 2 min → 30 s over weeks gained nearly identically to a fixed-2-min group [T23]
- Antagonist supersets (biceps/triceps, quads/hams) preserve performance and save time [T4, T23]
- Strength-context rests: 5–8 min between heavy sets; density training cuts to 2–3 min [T12]

## Training Frequency

- **Volume-equated meta-analyses: exact frequency barely matters** — it's a logistics/enjoyment decision [T1, T6, T18]
- Still, practical consensus: hit each muscle **at least 2×/week** [T1, T5, T18]; small/lagging muscles up to **3×/week** [T18, T9 context]
- Advanced trainees get a slight edge from 2–3×/week frequency [T18]
- Contrarian deep-dive (T22): frequency meta-analyses are misleading — they pool volumes too high for anyone to recover. **Low volume + high frequency = less damage = more of the MPS elevation builds new tissue.** High frequency with low per-session volume may grow fastest for advanced lifters.
- Distribution quality argument: 16 sets in one session vs 8+8 across two days — the split version has higher-quality volume [T18]
- More frequency per week → fewer sets per session; 1×/week → more sets per session [T2]

## Intensity/Load Bands (strength-sport framework, Andy Baker T12/T13/T14)

- **Heavy intensity:** 90–100% 1RM, 1–3 reps
- **Heavy volume:** 75–85% 1RM, 15–30 total reps (e.g., 5×5 @75% up to 5×3 @85%)
- **Light:** 60–70% 1RM, ~15 total reps (3×5, 4×4). NOT 10–12s @60–70% — that's still high-stress. Light day sets should never be missed.
- Strength-building range = 75–85%
- Heavy comes first in sessions, always (rare exception: light squats as deadlift warm-up)
- RIR/RPE alternative (T6): RPE 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 across a block (RPE 10 = failure); work at 1–3 RIR and log every rep

## Muscle-Specific Volume Notes (from batch 2, see 09–13)

- **Arms:** 3–6 direct sets/wk each (indirect back/press work counts); specialization up to 9–14 [arms/1, arms/3]
- **Traps (direct):** start 6 sets/wk (Kelso), progress to 12 if recovery allows [back/4]
- **Side delts:** 12–16 sets/wk over 3 days — the priority delt head [shoulders/5]
- **Back:** >25 working sets/wk = recovery risk for most [back/1]
- **Chest overlap:** pressing counts toward triceps (medial/lateral) and front delts; long-head triceps and side delts do NOT get covered by pressing [arms/1, shoulders/5]
- **Pressing recovery is the split bottleneck:** 4–5 sleeps between heavy press sessions once strong; upper back, quads, side delts, biceps, calves tolerate more frequency [back/7]
- **Resistance-profile × fatigue principle:** shortened-position (lockout) strength degrades fastest within a session; lengthened strength persists — sequence exercises accordingly [chest/3, back/6]

## Session Structure Rules

- Multi-joint/compound exercises first, smaller joints/isolations later [T4, T6] — unless a specific muscle/lift is a strength goal or priority (then it goes first while fresh) [T6]
- Warm-ups: 1–2 light back-to-back warm-up sets before the first exercise per muscle; don't count them as volume [T4, T5]
- Heavy work when fresh; reverse-pyramid early in session; pre-fatigue only if "heavy" means double-digit reps [T2, T3]

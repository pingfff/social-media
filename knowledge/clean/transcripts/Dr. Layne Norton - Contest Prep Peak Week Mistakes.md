# Contest Prep Peak Week Mistakes

98K views, 2.68% engagement, 11 min talking-head lecture, 2017.

## Key claims

- Credentials: 500+ prep clients over 10 years; signature = clients do NOT
  cut water or sodium.
- Homeostasis principle: manipulate many variables at once and the body
  pushes back — "you're not going to outwit a million years of evolution."
- Muscle water is ~70% intracellular vs ~30% extracellular/subcutaneous.
  Cutting water drains BOTH proportionally (Le Chatelier's principle:
  removing product C pulls A→B→C forward) — you lose more fullness than
  the sub-Q water you removed. Flat = soft.
- Cutting sodium is "one of the worst things you can do for stage look":
  ~30-year-old Harvard study — 6 days low sodium: blood sodium
  UNCHANGED, urinary sodium near zero, aldosterone (water-retention
  hormone) DOUBLED by day 2. Coaches cut sodium ~2 days out = aldosterone
  peaks exactly on show day; reabsorbing sodium drags water with it, and
  low-sodium-lowered blood pressure lets water leach into the interstitial
  layer. Net: water moves from muscle/vascular to subcutaneous — softer,
  flatter, less vascular, no pump.
- Sodium is also required for carb absorption: the intestinal glucose
  transporter is sodium-dependent; low sodium → carbs absorb worse →
  undigested carbs pull water into the gut → show-day bloating/GI
  distress.
- Post-show myth ("the cheat meal made me hard — I must have under-
  carbed"): what changed was water + sodium, not carbs. Any salty
  high-carb meal mid-prep makes you look hard/vascular ~2 h later.
- Fiber: eating broccoli backstage is counterproductive — fiber pulls
  water into the GI tract. Minimize fiber the final 24 h; use low-residue
  (low insoluble fiber), calorically dense carbs; keep the gut empty,
  waist tight. Fiber is great in general — just not show day.
- No tricks exist: no +50% glycogen, no +50% vascular fluid; diuretics
  can't pick a compartment. Job = arrive lean, make small tweaks, "make
  sure you don't screw them up."

## Mechanisms

- Le Chatelier's principle as compartment math: 70/30 split means water
  cuts hit muscle 2.3x harder than sub-Q.
- Aldosterone loop: sodium cut → aldosterone ↑ (2x by day 2) → sodium
  reabsorbed WITH water → retention peaks at the show.
- SGLT1 (sodium-dependent glucose transport) → sodium cuts sabotage the
  carb load itself.

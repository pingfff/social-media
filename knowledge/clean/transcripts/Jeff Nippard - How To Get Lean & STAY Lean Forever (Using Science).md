# How To Get Lean & STAY Lean Forever (Using Science)

## Key claims

- Diet failure data: Biggest Loser 6-year follow-up — only 1 of 14 kept
  weight off, 5 regained everything, 2 ended heavier than baseline.
  2020 systematic review (8 studies): regain after dieting is the norm,
  some with overshoot past starting weight.
- Fat loss = sustained deficit. Calories out = 4 components: REE, EAT,
  NEAT, TEF. 500 kcal/day deficit ≈ 0.5 kg/week.
- Metabolic adaptation: ALL four components drop as you diet (smaller
  body, more efficient exercise, less fidgeting, less TEF) — can happen
  "within days". Your starting deficit shrinks; adjust or accept slower
  loss. (Mechanism, not metabolic damage.)
- Non-negotiables: deficit + weight training + protein 1.6-2.2 g/kg.
  Meal count, timing, food choice = preference.
- Strategy 1 — diet SLOWLY: standard 0.5-1% bodyweight/week; he went
  slower: 11 kg in 40 weeks (85 -> 74 kg, ~0.25 kg/week), felt
  "ridiculously easy", ate out throughout; vacation spikes are blips in
  the trend. Deficit ~20% below maintenance. Loose tracking: calories +
  protein only, eyeball unknowns, ~5 min/day.
- Realistic end target: most men maintain 10-20% BF, women 18-28%.
  Sub-6% year-round tanks sleep, libido, mood. "Someone else's 8% might
  be your 18%."
- Strategy 2 — habits over motivation: temptation bundling (true-crime
  videos ONLY during cardio; podcast only during Sunday meal prep) +
  environment design (don't stock trigger foods; night stress-reliever
  alternatives; alarm clock instead of phone).
- Strategy 3 — post-diet plan (the neglected one): two failure modes =
  no plan at all (gradual creep back), or meticulous reverse dieting
  that drags out hunger -> willpower breakdown. His rec: jump STRAIGHT
  to new maintenance (~200-600 kcal above end-of-cut intake, higher if
  you didn't crash diet), then creep calories toward the TOP of your
  "dynamic maintenance range" (e.g. 2600-3000). More food = less
  restriction + better training. Keep weighing 2-3x/week (linked to
  better long-term maintenance).

## Craft notes

### Hook (verbatim)

> "if you click this video you've most likely set the goal of trying to
> lose some fat this year. this is my goal for the new year as well."

Why it works: self-selection (addresses the viewer by their own action —
clicking), then instant parasocial alignment ("me too") before any
advice. Stakes arrive 10 seconds later with evidence: "most diets fail
over the long term — this is true" (validates the viewer's doubt first,
never mocks it).

### Retention devices

- Curiosity gap placed BEFORE strategy 1, paid off after strategy 2:
  "the third strategy on my list is the most frequently neglected in my
  experience but also probably the most important" — you sit through
  two full strategies to collect the promised payoff.
- Time-boxed signpost: "give me one minute to explain how fat loss
  actually works" — asks for a small, explicit commitment before the
  mechanism primer.
- The "but" pivot after every simple block: "so that's all pretty
  simple but there's a very important part that many people Miss."
- Foreshadowing: "I'm planning to cover reverse dieting in detail in a
  future video" — open loop for the channel, not just this video.
- Problem agitation before the fix: paints two post-diet failure
  personas (no plan; meticulous reverse dieter) BEFORE revealing his
  recommendation.

### Rhetorical devices

- Concession anaphora on fad diets — three beats, escalating: "yes all
  the most popular fad diets will cause fat loss in the short term...
  but" / "yes isolating yourself from social events... but" / "yes
  cutting out entire food groups... but" — grants the premise, then
  flips it. Ideal myth-bust rhythm for the creator's brand.
- Second-person walkthrough with concrete numbers: "let's say you're
  eating 2000 calories by the end of your diet. The very next day you
  should boost your calories up to 2200 to 2600" — viewer simulates
  their own cut inside the video.
- Normalizing lapses with data: shows his own graph spikes (vacations,
  Thanksgiving) and reframes: "it's not a big deal at all when you zoom
  out and look at the overall trend" — permission-giving, removes
  diet-breaking shame.
- Calm authority markers: "this is true", "pretty much everything
  else", "the bottom line is" — no hype-bro energy, matches the
  creator's confident-calm-direct register.

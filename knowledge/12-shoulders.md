# Shoulder Training: Front, Side, Rear Delts

*Source: Program Design/shoulders/1–6 (front/side delt guides, Nippard+Israetel top-3, boulder-shoulders tips, rear-delt tier list w/ moment-arm science, Meadows shoulder tips)*

## Anatomy & Priorities

- Delts have **7 functional segments** (not just 3 heads) [shoulders/1, shoulders/5]
- **Side delts = the priority: 70–90% of delt volume** (Dr. Mike). "Big shoulders" = big side delts. German study: bodybuilders have 5× front-delt vs non-lifters but only 3× side-delt — pressing overbuilds front delts [shoulders/2]
- **Front delts:** mostly covered by chest pressing; direct work only if lagging [shoulders/1, shoulders/2]
- **Rear delts:** disputed — Israetel says "somewhat overrated" if back training is proper; others (Meadows, tier-list author) treat them as the #1 neglected priority (shoulders "slope down" without them). Resolve by individual assessment [shoulders/2 vs shoulders/3,4,6]
- **Overhead pressing does NOT build side delts** — activation ≠ hypertrophy; side delt just stabilizes, never taken through contractile range [shoulders/5]
- **Traps hijack any abducted/transverse-plane work** — that's normal scapulohumeral rhythm; limit ROM/control scaps to limit trap takeover [shoulders/4]

## Side Delts

**Programming:** 3×/week, 4–5 sets/session = **12–16 sets/week**; vary exercise across days; include at least one front-of-body entry and one behind-the-body entry (side delt also works raising from behind) [shoulders/5]

**Technique:**
- The delt segment FACING UP is the one working: keep side delt up → palm down / dumbbell pointed down; thumbs-up = front delt takes over [shoulders/5, shoulders/6]
- Train in the **scapular plane (~10° in front of frontal plane)** — impingement-free [shoulders/5]
- Cue: push dumbbells down, "scrape them up a wall"; cable alignment must run hand → elbow → shoulder joint [shoulders/5]
- Cross-body cable Y raise = chosen for the pre-stretch [shoulders/2]
- Behind-the-back/leaning raises = stretch position — prefer cables there (dumbbells lose lateral tension) [shoulders/6]
- Upright rows: viable if wide-grip + bar farther from body, but impingement-prone — rarely programmed [shoulders/5]
- Reps 12–25; failure via partials in the burn zone [shoulders/3]
- Prime machine cam protocol: first sets load lockout-heavy, final sets bottom-heavy → even reps across range [shoulders/5]

## Rear Delts (tier list, shoulders/4)

**S-tier:** lying incline DB raise (arms CLOSE to body, reach out, scoop back) — moment-arm science: in shoulder flexion, posterior delt beats lat; straight arms = more shoulder torque (less lat); arms close removes traps. Dual-cable arms-close fly (constant tension) also S. **#1 desert-island pick: single-arm cable rear-delt LENGTHENED PARTIALS.**
**A:** machine rear-delt fly converted to lengthened partials (scaps protracted + depressed, "wrap around ribcage," never elevated). Reverse pec deck with "paintbrush" cue: reach far forward, arc out as far as possible [shoulders/2, shoulders/4]
**F:** high dual-cable fly (arms overhead = full trap engagement) [shoulders/4]
- Cues: pinky-up/full rotation "door hinge" on reverse pec deck; last negative "as if doing another rep" [shoulders/2]; high-rep contraction work — "never about the weight" [shoulders/3]

## Front Delts

- **Incline DB press at 60–75°** (not 90° military): back brace to push into, less external rotation, adduction allowed, big bottom stretch; hands ~45°, CONVERGING as you press ("up and across") [shoulders/1]
- Isolation (if needed): single-arm cable front raises — "up and across" (90° pulley, arm starts behind body) and "up and out" scaption version (cable through hand→elbow→shoulder) [shoulders/1]
- EZ-bar front raise: effective range = 45–135° of flexion; going to the floor "wastes half a slice of pizza"; chest up throughout [shoulders/2]

## Sequencing & Session Design

- **Rear delts FIRST in every shoulder session** (underdeveloped, safe to prioritize), then side delts, then press [shoulders/1, shoulders/6]
- Weak-delt specialization: delts 3×/week all priority-first — rear + side in every session, press in 2, front-delt raise in 1 [shoulders/1]
- Alternative (shoulders/3): rear + side first so they become the limiter when you press; include one standing press for athletics
- Meadows: press not required for big shoulders if lateral work is done well (long-term joint wear concern); pairs chest+shoulders in same program to avoid double heavy pressing; partial pressing (cut depth) = 15–20 years of results + pain relief for some clients; against behind-neck pressing [shoulders/6]
- Intensity: myo-rep match sets (set 1 to failure sets the rep target; sets 2–3 match it with ~5-s intra-set rests) [shoulders/3]; heavy-partial + light-top-partial supersets [shoulders/6]
- Shoulder health: band over-and-backs (10–15, progressively narrower grip), spider crawls against a short band [shoulders/6]

## Contradictions Logged

- Rear delts "overrated" [shoulders/2] vs "start every session with them" [shoulders/3,4,6]
- Failure-every-set + 25-rep laterals + 100 daily raises [shoulders/3] vs moderate RIR baseline — outlier enthusiasm, use selectively
- Partials with 30s DBs for 20–30+ reps ("sets of 60") [shoulders/6] — top-of-range intensity technique
- Pinky-up cue for laterals [shoulders/2,3] vs palm-down/side-delt-up [shoulders/5,6] — resolved as: orient so SIDE delt faces up; pinky-up risks impingement for some

# Chest Training

*Source: Program Design/chest/1–3 (10 tips video, sternum-angle individualization, pro push-day resistance-profile system)*

## Anatomy & Individual Structure

- **Sternum angle varies by person and changes what "incline" means** [chest/2]:
  - Steep sternum angle → flat bench acts like a DECLINE → need **30–45° incline** for upper chest
  - Flat sternum angle → only a **very slight incline** for upper chest; more incline shifts to front delts
  - Self-test: lie on flat bench, phone on sternum between pecs — angles down = steep; horizontal = flat
- 2020 study: incline-only training = equal mid-chest growth + significantly MORE upper-chest growth vs flat [chest/2]
- Stretch-loaded work grows distal muscle regions preferentially (trend: outer chest) [chest/2]

## Exercise Selection & Reasoning

- **Smith machine / machine presses ≥ barbell for hypertrophy**: safe to push to/past failure → more effective reps; constant tension [chest/1]
- **Cambered bar press** (or DBs pressed below chest level) = deepest stretch; deep ROM beats half-reps [chest/1]
- **Dumbbell incline press** fixes "barbell bench non-responders": barbell locks arm path, fighting some shoulder/rib structures; DBs allow path adjustment [chest/2]
- **Incline cable fly** (setup: bench one notch below highest, cables at shoulder height, butt forward, foam roller behind back, forearms in line with cables) = stretch + tension through the WHOLE ROM including the squeeze — something presses can't do [chest/2]
- Pec deck: full reps to failure → then **stretch-position half reps** to failure (half reps in the stretch beat half reps at the top — demonstrated in biceps/quads/calves; chest pending) [chest/2]
- Machine setup: handles far back, at/just above nipple height [chest/1]
- **Pre-exhaust (flies before pressing) is counterproductive for ~99.99% of lifters** — less load = less tension. Exception: very advanced lifters where heavy compound stimulus-to-fatigue is poor [chest/1]

## Resistance-Profile Management (advanced, chest/3)

- Muscles retain LENGTHENED-position strength best; shortened (lockout) strength degrades fastest with fatigue
- Early sets can load shortened position; every subsequent set should shift load toward lengthened profile
- Plate-loaded machine peg strategy: split load across pegs to even the profile; shift toward the lengthened-loading peg as sets accumulate
- Hammer Strength-style (shortened-loaded) machines = acceptable as FIRST exercise only; obsolete late in session
- Late-session triceps/chest picks: lengthened-loaded options (single-arm overhead extension, drop-off-short machines)
- Reverse banding criticized: unloads the lengthened position where you're strongest — backwards
- **Dead-stop reps** (Smith/dip machine): removes stretch reflex, standardizes reps, re-concentrates intent — alternate stints of dead-stop and normal pressing

## Programming Numbers

- **2 chest sessions/week**, volume split; rejects single 25-set "international chest day" [chest/1]
- Session pattern [chest/1]: press (rotating incline-first / flat-first) → secondary press at different angle → 2–3 sets flies or dips
- Two exercises can build the whole chest (dumbbell incline press + incline cable fly) if form + progression are right [chest/2]
- Pro push session [chest/3]: ~12 working sets total — 6 compound sets (chest bias via angle: chest-first incline, high-incline shoulder tie-in, narrow-grip tricep/decline bias), 2 pec deck sets, side delts 5, triceps 2; chest ~every 4–5 days
- Reps: hypertrophy 8–12 on compounds; isolations 6–8 first set, slightly higher later; ~5–30 works to failure [chest/3]
- Progression pacing example: dropped 95-lb DBs → 65s for proper stretch form, took ~1 YEAR back to 80s — never sacrifice form for load [chest/2]

## Technique Cues & Mistakes

- **Ronnie Coleman-style press:** upper arms well below 90° at bottom (deep stretch), do NOT fully lock out at top [chest/2]
- Pause every rep at the bottom; slow eccentric; explode up [chest/1]
- "Chest to the sky" cue; "string pulling the sternum up"; squeeze biceps into sides of chest — when shoulders creep forward, chest has failed: END THE SET [chest/1, chest/2]
- Lockout weakness on bench = triceps, not chest — strengthen triceps [chest/1]
- Partials past failure only on last 1–2 sets of a couple movements (very taxing) [chest/1]
- Pec deck: drive humerus/upper arms together — closing hands ≠ shortening the pec; don't lean into reps [chest/3]

## Contradictions Logged

- chest/1 and chest/3 run 0 RIR / failure (safely, on machines) vs baseline 2–3 RIR — machine-safety justification [chest/1, chest/3]
- Low direct volume (~8–11 chest-stimulating sets/session counting overlap, every 4–5 days) vs 10–20/week — overlap counting matters [chest/3]
- "All angles are good, rotate which leads" [chest/1] vs "incline-first for upper chest" [chest/2] — resolved by sternum-angle individualization

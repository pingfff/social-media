# Leg Training: Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves

*Source: Program Design/legs/1–3 (Nippard 365-day leg day, Nippard PPL lower day, Joe Linhardt leg architecture)*

## Anatomy & Head-Specific Targeting

### Quads
- **Rectus femoris** crosses hip AND knee → can't fully activate in squats/lunges (hips extend as knees extend); grows ONLY from leg extensions (fixed hip). 2021 study: 5 weeks of Smith squats did NOT grow rectus femoris; extensions did. Include extensions ≥1×/week [legs/1, legs/2]
- **Vastus lateralis (sweep):** knee toward midline relative to hip — lunges, step-ups, toes slightly in [legs/3]
- **VMO (teardrop):** full ROM — deep squats, cannonball squats, high-box step-ups, terminal knee extensions [legs/3]
- **Vastus intermedius:** grows from any high-tension squat work, no special needs [legs/3]

### Hamstrings (two functions — train BOTH)
- **Knee flexion:** leg curls. **Seated > lying for growth (~1.6×, 12-wk study)** because seated = more lengthened; but LYING stretches the sartorius → split seated/lying across leg days [legs/1]
- **Hip extension:** RDLs/stiff-legs — complements curls; also the big erector/back-builder [legs/1, legs/2, legs/6]
- Head bias: biceps femoris (lateral) = toes in/femur rotated in on curls; medial hamstrings = toes out [legs/3]
- Nordic curls/GHR = highest-tension hamstring builders (4–5×4–7) [legs/3]

### Glutes
- Glute max: squats/RDLs/thrusts; glute **medius**: frontal-plane work + hips-EXTENDED abduction position (bridged = glute max; extended = medius; medius fibers poorly aligned in leaned-forward seated abduction) [legs/1, legs/3]
- **Adductor magnus** = huge hip extensor, "meatier thighs": Cossack squats, wide-stance squats, frog-stance deadlifts [legs/3]

### Calves
- Standing/straight-leg = gastrocnemius; seated/bent-knee = soleus. BUT soleus responds poorly regardless of rep range (2020) — "high reps for slow-twitch soleus" logic is outdated; standing work is reasonable as a replacement [legs/2]
- Deep stretch under load is the calf growth driver; finish with a **30-s bottom-stretch hold** [legs/1]
- Counter-view [legs/3]: calves largely grow from life load (rucking, sprinting, hiking); gym calf work 1×/week mainly for Achilles/foot health. Donkey calf raise = favorite.

## Exercise Priorities & Architecture (Linhardt framework, legs/3)

**Four growth levers that built his legs in his 30s:**
1. More frequency — legs every **3–5 days** (not 7); 8–9-day microcycle; 1×/week hard leg days wreck life via soreness
2. Prioritize UNILATERAL work: Bulgarian split squats, walking lunges, step-ups — "most complete leg exercises," done FIRST while fresh, with real weight. **Unilateral = ≥20%, ideally 30–40% of leg volume**
3. More hamstring energy — Nordics/GHR; glutes+hams deserve ~50% of leg-training energy (typical gym legs are 90% quad-dominant)
4. High-rep squatting — 10–20 reps, full ROM, slowed tempo; depth > load for growth

- **Frontal-plane work ≥10–20% of leg volume** (Cossack lunges, lateral walks)
- Heavy axial hinges (deadlifts, >80% 1RM, 3–6 reps): only **1–2×/month** as openers — axial/CNS cost limits frequency, especially with age
- Alternate leg days: (A) unilateral opener → unloaded hinge (45° back extension) → high-rep squat → extensions + lateral walks → seated curl; (B) heavy hinge OR SSB/zercher/hack opener → lighter unilateral → reverse hypers → Nordics → Cossack

## Nippard's Leg-Day Templates

**Day 1 (legs/1):** lying leg curl 3 (lengthened partials on last set; curl-first pre-activates posterior chain — "cranky knees → curl first") → pendulum squat 3×6–8 (#1 quad builder; subs: Smith feet-forward, hack, high-bar squat) → RDL ~6 hard reps → leg extension 1–2 (seat set back = ~170% more growth; strap hips down) → abduction pushed to max load (if you don't need to sit 10 s after, too easy) → straight-leg calves 8–10 + 30-s stretch hold

**Day 2 (legs/2):** conventional DL 1×5 top set @ RPE 8–9 (linear periodization 5→1 reps over 5 weeks optional; hypertrophy-only: stay at 5) → SLDL 2×8 (~50–60% of DL top set) → leg press 4×10–12 (no lockout, continuous tension, rest capped 2 min) → GHR 3×8–10 (active range — cut top quarter where tension = 0; subs: Nordic curls) → slow-eccentric (3 s) leg extension 3×8–10 → seated calf 4×15–20 → roman-chair leg raises 3×10–20

## Technique Cues & Mistakes

- Leg curls: drive hips into pad, grip handles — no hip pop [legs/1]
- Squat/press depth: quad torque highest at bottom; film from the side; depth improvements grew him more than load [legs/1, legs/3]
- RDL: pure hinge, glutes straight back, bar over mid-foot, only to just below knee with neutral spine [legs/1, legs/2]
- Leg extension: seat back as far as possible, glutes pinned [legs/1]
- Abduction: rotate hip angles across time to hit all fibers; treat as a strength lift, not pump work [legs/1]
- Intensity philosophy: "quads grow with pain" — push past the breathe-hard stop point; glutes/hams need high TENSION, quads need volume + discomfort [legs/3]
- Mistake pattern: all-bilateral, 90% quad-dominant, 1×/week, sagittal-only, axial loading too often [legs/3]

## Contradictions Logged

- legs/3 de-emphasizes gym calf volume (life-load theory) vs legs/1's dedicated calf protocol — context: athlete vs bodybuilder
- Hinges 1–2×/month [legs/3] vs every leg day (legs/1, legs/2) — axial recovery budget is individual
- Failure on last sets of machines [legs/1] vs 2–3 RIR baseline — machine-safety framing again

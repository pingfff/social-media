# Training Principles & Hypertrophy Science

*Source: transcripts 1, 2, 3, 4, 18, 22, 23*

## The Primary Growth Driver: Mechanical Tension

- **Mechanical tension is THE primary (arguably sole) driver of hypertrophy.** This is described as "undisputed" [T1, T3, T4].
- The older 3-factor model (mechanical tension + metabolic stress + muscle damage) comes from Brad Schoenfeld's 2010 landmark paper. It has been revised: tension = ~100% of the stimulus; damage and metabolic stress are "passengers," not drivers [T1, T4].
- **Muscle damage is NOT a growth driver.** Research (Schoenfeld review, Damas et al.) shows damage doesn't correlate with long-term hypertrophy; early protein synthesis goes to repair, not new growth [T1]. Muscle damage actually *downregulates* recruitment via inflammatory negative feedback — damaged tissue reduces force production and the brain throttles motor unit recruitment [T22].
- **The pump / metabolic stress is NOT a growth driver.** Evidence: shorter rest → more metabolic stress but LESS growth; partial ROM in shortened positions → more metabolites, less growth; BFR works via tension, not metabolites [T1].
- Caveat: the effective-reps model is incomplete — e.g., submaximal high-volume training (Sheiko-style pyramids) still grows muscle [T2].

## Molecular Cascade (how tension becomes growth)

- Mechanosensors (costameres, titin, filamins — candidates) → mTOR → mRNA → ribosomes → muscle protein synthesis → myofibrillar hypertrophy [T1].
- Leucine (~3g leucine via ~20–25g quality protein) activates mTOR, but the training stimulus is the longer-acting driver [T1].
- Sarcoplasmic hypertrophy has some evidence; myonuclear addition is speculative [T1].
- **Effective reps model:** ~the last 5 reps before failure are the growth-stimulating ones — slow bar speed + maximal intent recruits high-threshold motor units [T2, T21, T22].

## The Effective Reps Framework (Mike Israetel / T22 "weekly net stimulus")

- 1 hard set to failure ≈ 1 "unit" of hypertrophy stimulus.
- 2 sets ≠ 2 units — it takes roughly **5–6 additional sets to produce another full unit**; per-session stimulus plateaus around **~6 hard sets per muscle per session** (with long rest) [T22].
- Per-set effectiveness decays within a session — every set after the first is less effective; ~20 sets of one muscle in a single session = severe diminishing returns [T18].
- Volume lands on an inverted-U dose-response curve: biggest jumps come from the first 1–5 weekly sets; returns level off around 15–16 sets per muscle per session [T23].

## Protein & Nutrition Basics

- **Protein: 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day (0.7–1 g/lb)** [T1]. For very high body fat: ~1 g per cm of height [T1].
- Spread across 3–5 meals possibly optimal, but daily total matters far more [T1].
- Training matters far more than diet for growth stimulus [T1].
- Creatine supports short-rest performance by increasing phosphocreatine stores and resynthesis rate (+5% power in cited study) [T23].

## Key Scientific/Physiological Facts

- **Strength ≠ hypertrophy.** Strength is neural/specific; you can gain strength without mass (MMA athletes). 5, 12, and 20–30 reps build equal muscle **if within ~1 rep of failure** [T4, T1].
- **Atrophy is fast and binary:** once muscle protein synthesis returns to baseline and goes negative, high-threshold motor unit fibers shrink. ICU patients lost ~1% of vastus lateralis per day. ~10 days out of the gym = real detraining tipping point [T22, T15].
- **Muscle overlap/maintenance stimulus:** related movements provide residual stimulus — lat pulldowns hit pecs at top ROM; all pressing covers lateral/medial triceps heads; this overlap can maintain a muscle within the week without direct work [T22].
- **Stretch-mediated hypertrophy:** the lengthened position > shortened position. The stretched part of the rep is more important than the squeezed part [T2, T3, T4]. Lengthened partials can beat full ROM in some cases [T3].
- Genetics follow a bell curve — champions are outliers across multiple domains; individual response to training varies widely [T2].

## Rep Range Truth (myths busted)

- "6–12 rep hypertrophy zone" is a **myth** — 3 to 30 reps produce similar growth when taken close to failure [T1, T4, T6, T2 (Haney-era "sculpting" rep-ranges are superstition)].
- Below ~20% 1RM (very long sets, 60+ reps) → significantly less growth [T1].
- Practical guidance on 6–12: <6 risks joints and volume accumulation is harder; >12 is fatiguing/harder to recover from. Hence: majority of sets 6–12 (or up to 15) is a *practical* choice, not a biological requirement [T1].
- Rep range does not change muscle structure — "heavy for mass, light for sculpt" is superstition [T2].

## Effort & Intensity

- **Effort (proximity to failure) is the most universally important factor** [T2].
- Most people don't push hard enough [T3]. Two 2022 meta-analyses conflict: one finds growth spikes at 0 RIR vs 2 RIR; another finds no evidence failure training is superior [T3].
- Practical prescriptions across sources:
  - Don't leave more than **2–3 RIR** on most sets [T1].
  - **Last set to failure, first sets 1–2 RIR** (2–3 sets per exercise) [T3].
  - Leave reps in the tank on heavy free-weight compounds (squat/bench/dead) even on the final set — disproportionate fatigue [T3].
  - Training within 1 rep of failure makes 5–30 reps roughly equivalent [T4].
  - Contrarian: T21 (hardcore natural coach) **rejects RIR entirely** — "you've got to train to failure"; he claims RIR-style training made him regress. Known outlier view.
- Failure on heavy compounds costs disproportionate systemic fatigue → prefer **low-fatigue exercises** (machines, cables) when training close to failure [T3, T4].
- One contrarian defense of short rests and damage: soreness is used by some coaches as a progress marker, though science says soreness ≠ growth [T19, T18].

## Tempo, ROM, Technique

- **Tempo: 2–4 s controlled negative, forceful/explosive concentric** [T3]. Beyond ~5 s eccentric adds nothing (10 s = no more growth than 2 s) [T4].
- Eccentrics are "likely more important" for growth than concentrics; the last reps of a set are the most hypertrophic — control the negative especially on final reps [T3].
- Full ROM generally; at least parallel on squats; deep stretch on presses/pulls [T3]. Tension at end-range (lengthened) matters most [T4].
- Stretched-position partials sometimes beat full ROM [T3].
- No excessive cheating/momentum — keep tension on the target muscle [T3].
- Explosive concentric + controlled eccentric with constant tension may be optimal (Ronnie Coleman-style lifting gets a sympathetic modern read) [T2].
- Mind-muscle connection can increase hypertrophy in some cases, especially isolation work [T3].

## What Actually Drives Long-Term Results

- **Progression is king.** "It's the progression from week to week that drives progress, not monumental effort in any one workout" [T2, T16].
- Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio matters (term from Mike Israetel): the best exercise gives maximal stimulus for minimal fatigue [T3, T4].
- The logbook moving up over time is the ultimate indicator — not soreness, not the pump [T18, T19].
- If you're progressing, it's the right amount of work (volume is judged by progress, not feelings) [T16].
- Consistency and enjoyment > theoretical optimality — "the best split is the one that suits you in the moment" [T19, T20, T21, T22].

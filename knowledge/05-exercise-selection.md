# Exercise Selection

*Source: transcripts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 17, 22, 23*

## Core Rules

- **No mandatory exercises.** The muscle doesn't know what exercise you're doing — it only knows the stimulus [T1, T2, T5, T6]. Experiment to find what fits your mechanics [T1].
- **First filter = injury/pain.** Swap or modify any painful exercise — never train through joint pain [T6].
- **Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio** is the selection criterion (Mike Israetel's term): deadlift = high stimulus but very high fatigue; machines/cables give large tension with less fatigue and are safer near failure [T3, T4].
- Machines ≥ free weights for hypertrophy in research (possibly superior): smooth resistance profiles and tension in the stretch (e.g., face-away cable curl vs dumbbell curl where tension ≈ 0 at the bottom) [T3]. Free weights keep advantages: accessibility, strength carryover, stabilizer work [T3].
- Compound vs isolation doesn't matter with volume equated [T6]. Compounds are practically convenient (bench hits triceps too); isolation adds targeted volume for lagging muscles [T6].
- Favor multi-joint compounds for "bang for buck," but isolation is REQUIRED for biceps, rear/side delts, abs [T1].

## The Lengthened Position (stretch-first selection)

- Choose exercises that load the muscle in its **lengthened position** — the stretch is more hypertrophic than the squeeze [T3, T4]:
  - Preacher or incline curls (tension at the bottom) vs standard curl
  - Full squat > 90° squat for quads
  - Deep-stretch presses and pull-ups
  - Leg press: feet low & narrow = deep knee angle = better quad stimulus; feet high/wide = more glute/hip
  - Flex Wheeler chest fix: DB press with sternum up, deep elbow stretch — more chest growth in 6 months than 20 years prior
- If an exercise has no deep stretch: go deeper, fix mobility, or swap it [T3].

## Exercise Order

- Multi-joint first, smaller joints/isolations later [T4, T6]
- Exception: whatever you're prioritizing (a lift for strength, a lagging muscle) goes first while fresh [T6, T8 — "priority to weakest point first"]
- Default push day: chest → shoulders → triceps [T21]
- Compounds-first overall; stretched-position holds / pump finishers last [T5, T23]
- In upper/lower design, alternate push-biased and back-biased upper days; use chest-braced back work to avoid fatigue interference [T17]

## Volume Distribution Across Exercises

- **Max ~5 sets per exercise per session**; more muscle volume → add another exercise at a different angle [T6]
- Larger muscles benefit from multiple exercises/angles (e.g., vertical + horizontal pull) [T6]
- Prefer 2 near-failure sets × 3 exercises over 4 sets × 1 exercise [T4]

## Proven Substitutions & Swaps

- Machines are interchangeable — "don't wait for the machine, pick a close alternative" [T5]
- Chest: incline Smith/machine/DB press ↔ each other; pec deck/crossover as finisher [T5]
- Back: single-arm cable row ↔ straight-arm pulldown; chest-supported row for upper back; cross-body lat pulldown for stretch [T3, T5]
- Arms: Bayesian curl ↔ incline DB curl; hammer curl variations; cable triceps extension (rope/cross-cable), overhead extension, weighted dips [T5, T8]
- Legs: hack ↔ Smith ↔ leg press (foot placement changes emphasis) [T5, T17]; Pit Shark/barbell/Smith SLDL interchangeable [T5]
- Strength-context swaps: rack pulls before heavy deads = bad (hard to recover); use SLDL/RDL/power clean instead [T14]; front squat favored after heavy deadlifts [T14]
- Weak-point targeting via accessory selection (conjugate): sticking-point analysis — bottom-of-bench weakness → upper back work; deadlift lockout weakness → glutes/hams [T9]

## Muscle Overlap & Maintenance Logic [T22]

- Triceps: only the **long head** typically needs direct work — pressing covers lateral/medial heads (pullover extension, cross-body extension, one-arm pushdown)
- Lat pulldowns with full ROM contribute meaningful pec stimulus
- Overhead press in the sagittal plane hits a lot of pec; scaption plane hits some
- This overlap lets you maintain muscles with reduced direct volume — a key tool in split design

## Specific Program Structures (as given)

**T5 — 5-day split (ham leg / chest push / back pull / rest / quad leg / full upper / rest):**
- Warm-ups: 2 back-to-back sets before first exercise per muscle
- Leg A (ham): seated leg curl 3×8–12; Smith/hack 3×6–10 (feet high); SLDL 2–3 sets; optional BSS 2 sets; abductors 3
- Push: incline press 3; flat machine press 3; lateral raise 4×10–15; triceps extension + cable ext; pec deck 2
- Pull: pulldown 3×8–12; chest-supported row 3; hammer curl 2–3; single-arm row 3; Bayesian curl
- Leg B (quad): hack feet low 3×6–10; leg extension 3×12–15; adduction; calves on step
- Upper: incline 3, flat 2, 2 lat exercises, lateral raises 4, biceps 3–4, triceps 3–4; alternate chest-first/back-first weekly

**T8 — Torso/limb (6-day) template:**
- Torso days: press pair + fly, vertical pull ×2, horizontal row, lateral raise, rear delts
- Limb days: arms first (preacher curl + triceps ext), leg curl, quad extension, leg press, hinge, calves

## Beginner-Specific Notes

- Novices need repeated sets to groove technique — one all-out set (5/3/1 style) gives too few bar touches [T16]
- Blunt heavy compound work is very effective but taxing/injury-prone — good for beginners [T2]
- "Blunt and basic" first; exercise variation is a tool, not a necessity — master the basics [T2]

# Recovery & Fatigue Management

*Source: transcripts 4, 9, 10, 11, 13, 15, 17, 18, 20, 22, 23*

## Rest Days & Scheduling

- **48–72 h between hard sessions of the same muscle groups** [T9]; ~72 h between max-effort-style sessions [T10, T11]
- Only "super athletes" genuinely recover in 24 h — most can't compress schedules [T11]
- Place your hardest sessions after rest days (e.g., both leg days after rest days was a winning 5-day split design) [T5]
- Deadlifts: schedule before the most rest days (Friday with weekend off) — highest CNS drain [T14]
- Lower-body days drain the nervous system ~24 h → order upper days first in the week [T14]
- Bro-split scheduling rule: never more than 3 training days in a row without a rest day [T20]
- Rest-day rotations: hybrid PPL (fixed Sun/Wed off) balances rest and consistency [T20]
- "The easiest way to dissipate fatigue is more days off" — the week is a social construct; you can roll programs across 8–10-day cycles [T15]

## Fatigue Types

- **Localized fatigue:** per-set effectiveness decays within a session; a muscle hammered with ~20 sets in one session gets severe diminishing returns [T18]
- **Systemic fatigue:** whole-body "brain fog" degrades entire sessions [T18]
- **Muscle damage downregulates recruitment:** damage-driven inflammation throttles motor unit recruitment; strength can take >4 days to return to baseline after damaging protocols [T22]
- High-frequency low-volume training minimizes damage so more recovery capacity builds new tissue [T22]

## Detecting Under-Recovery

Warning signs requiring intervention [T15, T20, T9]:
- Feeling beat up / worn down; getting sick often
- Losing motivation to train
- Staying sore longer than usual
- Light weights feeling heavy
- Can't get a pump
- Declining performance / joint pain
- Overtraining is real for hard-training people on 2×/week body-part frequency; average gym-goers don't hit it because they don't train hard enough [T20]

## Deloads

**Purpose:** stress drives adaptation but accumulates background fatigue; periodic deloads dissipate it. Never add new stress during a deload — training should feel easy. **If your light deload week still feels hard, you timed it right** [T15].

**Match the deload to the program's primary stressor [T15]:**
- **Volume-driven programs:** cut volume 25–75% (**~50% is the sweet spot**), keep intensity nearly intact (5–10% drop only). "Volume lets you recover, intensity preserves your strength."
- **Intensity-driven programs:** slash intensity to ~60% 1RM, possibly raise volume slightly (3×5 vs one top set)
- **Conjugate (both):** halve sets of volume components (keep weights); cap max effort at 80–85% or skip it. **Nothing ≥90% on a deload week.**
- **Frequency deload (foolproof):** change nothing in workouts — spread the week over 8–12 days (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri/Mon; or a 5-day split across ~10 days). Many clients adopted this permanently.
- **Localized deloads:** remove stressors for overworked areas — lower back (swap bent-over → chest-supported rows, cut SLDLs/rack pulls/deadlift volume), elbows (cut triceps extensions, dips, CGBP), shoulders (barbell bench → DBs or narrow grip)
- Full week off: fine a couple of times per year. **10 days off = detraining tipping point** [T15, T22]
- Deload frequency: typically every 4–12 weeks — by schedule or by symptoms [T15, T6, T16]
- Novices and early 5/3/1 cycles don't need deloads — insufficient accumulated stress [T16]

## Prep/Deficit Considerations

- In a caloric deficit (~1,500 kcal + 12k steps/day), dedicated leg days wreck recovery for days — distribute leg volume across the week as single movements instead [T7]
- Keep at least 1 heavy top single at high RPE on the big lifts to maintain strength [T7]
- Autoregulate: do less when feeling trashed [T7]
- Post-contest: drop to 4-day U/L at maintenance volume to restore recovery, then rebuild [T17]

## Recovery Supports

- Nutrition (adequate protein — see 01), sleep, and stress management are repeatedly emphasized as non-negotiable [T9, T10]
- Creatine supports inter-set recovery (phosphocreatine resynthesis) [T23]
- Light activity/steps on rest days [T5]
- Watch for the program being unrunnable: "if a program can't flex around life events → fracture. Adapt, don't break" [T10]

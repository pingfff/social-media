# Program Styles & Splits

*Source: transcripts 2, 5, 7, 8, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23*

## The Progression Ladder (most consistent finding across sources)

**Full body → Upper/Lower → PPL → Body-part (bro) split**, advancing when:
- Sessions run past ~1–1.5 h and quality of later exercises/secondary muscles drops [T17, T21]
- Lifts in the back half of sessions stall from cumulative fatigue [T21]
- You can no longer recover between sessions of the current split

Approximate timelines [T21]:
- Full body: ~12–18 months
- Upper/lower: another ~18 months
- After ~3 years total you may have gained ~30 lb lean tissue — only then are real weak points identifiable

**Important nuances:**
- A split can also be a REGRESSION: if someone trains beyond their per-set execution ability, moving from PPL down to U/L (or a PPL+U/L hybrid) can improve results by concentrating stimulus quality [T17]
- Full body is wrongly branded "beginners only" (60s–70s drug-era stigma) — advanced lifters can use it with specialization [T22]
- Don't pick a split then force volume into it — **your volume-per-muscle needs dictate the split, not the reverse** [T7, T17]

## Split-by-Split

### Full Body 3×/week
- Mathematically highest "weekly net stimulus" [T22]
- Beginners: "a little bit of stimulus does a lot" — 1 set grows them [T17]
- Margin for error is smallest; requires smart volume caps (1–3 sets to failure per exercise, ~6 sets/muscle/session plateau) [T22]
- Old-school naturals used low-volume full body 3×/week [T2, T22]

### Upper/Lower (4 days: e.g., Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri)
- The sweet spot for intermediates — near-full-body stimulus with weekend off and flexibility via muscle overlap [T22]
- Session design template (J3U, T17):
  - Day 1 Upper: push-biased (push first), all back work chest-braced
  - Day 1 Lower: hamstring-focused, quads late
  - Day 2 Upper: back-biased first, push second
  - Day 2 Lower: quad-focused, non-axial loading (hack squat), hamstrings via leg curls (no hinges)
- Key design constraint: **fatigue carryover between adjacent sessions** — heavy back day before a hinge-heavy lower day is the most common collision (lower-back fatigue) [T17]
- Female competitors (wellness/bikini/figure): typically 3 lowers + 1 upper [T17]
- Specialization: cut upper to one bare-bones maintenance day, reallocate to legs [T17]
- 4-day strength version (T13/T14): volume upper / volume lower / rest / intensity upper / intensity lower

### Push/Pull/Legs (PPL)
- Rotation options [T20, T21]:
  - **Synchronous**: fixed weekly (PPL rest PPL, Sunday off) — 6 days in a row, 1 rest/week
  - **Asynchronous**: PPL then 1 off day, rotating — more rest days but inconsistent weekdays
  - **Hybrid (T20 favorite)**: Push, Pull, OFF, Legs, Push, Pull, OFF — rest days fixed (Sun/Wed)
  - **3 on 1 off continuous** — ≈2×/week frequency per muscle [T19]
  - T21's preferred: Push, Pull, REST, Legs, REST (each muscle ~every 5 days)
- PPL = "advanced" split: more volume per system, all recovery resources go to what you trained, but 6-day weeks risk overrunning recovery [T20, T22]
- Push day order default: **chest first, shoulders second, triceps third** [T21]
- Emphasis alternation across repeated days: quad-glute leg day vs hamstring-calf leg day; lat-biased back day vs heavy-movement back day; alternate biceps/triceps head emphasis [T19]
- If pull work can't progress after push in U/L → move to PPL [T21]

### Bro Split / Body-Part Split (each muscle ~1×/week)
- Works if: sessions are brutal, high-effort, not filled with junk volume [T19, T20]
- Failure mode for advanced trainees: stimulus → recovery → adaptation **regresses before the next session** (7-day gap) → plateau [T18]
- T21's failed experiment: 2-on-1-off bro split = each muscle once every 10–11 days = regression. "Training things once every 10 days is way too long."
- Defensible when: schedule demands (only ~45 min/session → body-part days), personal preference/consistency, or elite-level weak-point focus [T19, T20, T21]
- Historical giants ran bro splits effectively (Arnold, Dorian, Ronnie) — with drugs, extreme effort, and genetics [T2, T20, T22]
- Modifications: run it 5 days with an arm day + extra frequency dose for lagging parts (hybrid) [T18]

### Torso/Limb Split (T8 template)
- 4 sessions run twice per week (6 training days); arms trained first on limb days + indirectly on torso days = arms 4×/week with no dedicated arm day
- 2–3 hard sets per exercise; 6–10 reps compounds, 10–15 isolation; run 12 weeks

### Conjugate (Westside, strength-biased) [T9, T10, T11]
- 4 days: Max Effort Lower (Mon), Max Effort Upper (Wed), Dynamic Effort Lower (Fri), Dynamic Effort Upper (Sat/Sun)
- Max effort: work to 1RM in ~7 sets, rotate main lift weekly, one upper + one lower max-effort day/week
- Dynamic lower: 3-week pendulum wave — 12×2 @50% → 55% → 60% (+25% bands/chains; 75/80/85% without)
- Dynamic upper: 9×3 @~35% (+25% bands/chains), different press variation weekly (3 grips close/medium/wide)
- Repetition/accessory: 3–5 × 8–12, targets individual weaknesses
- 72 h between sessions rule; accessory rotation every 3–4 weeks
- Best for strength/power/speed — NOT a hypertrophy-first system

### 5/3/1 (Wendler) [T16]
- 4-day lift-per-day (squat/bench/dead/OHP); weekly wave 85%×5+ → 90%×3+ → 95%×1+ off a 90% training max
- +5–10 lb per 4-week cycle; BBB accessory = 5×10
- Critique: slow by design, too little volume/technique practice for novices, mandatory 4-week deloads are overcautious early
- Solid foundational program; won't take you to elite numbers

### Specialized/adjacent structures
- **HLM (Heavy-Light-Medium)**: 3-day full-body intermediate; only heavy day maximal; medium day ~5–10% below heavy, light ~10–20% below [T13]
- **Texas Method**: heavy-light-heavy; volume day 5×5 AND intensity day both maximal — hardest to recover from [T12, T13]
- **"Non-restricted" / inside-out splits** (T7): distribute per-muscle volume targets across 5–6 days without dedicated leg days — used in contest prep for fatigue management (e.g., legs spread as 1 movement per day × 3 sets)
- **PPL + off + Upper/Lower hybrids** — the preferred "regression that progresses stimulus delivery" [T17]

## Decision Heuristics for Choosing a Split

1. **Consistency rules.** The split you'll actually follow beats the theoretically optimal one [T19, T20, T21, T22]
2. Check current program's per-muscle volume FIRST; never drop volume by switching [T17]
3. Sessions too long / back-half quality decaying → advance up the ladder [T17, T21]
4. Fresh at end of every session and fully recovered → you can regress/specialize [T17]
5. Time-capped (~45 min/session) → body-part or 2-muscle-group days [T21]
6. Advanced: fixed recoverable set budget — adding volume to a lagging part requires cutting volume elsewhere [T18]
7. Fatigue overlap: don't schedule heavy back before hinge-heavy leg day; manage push-vs-back-first upper days [T17]
8. Strength-sport athletes/older trainees → HLM-style distributed stress over TM-style maximal days [T13]

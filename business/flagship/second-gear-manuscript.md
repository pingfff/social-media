## Page 2 — Who this is for

**This program is for you if:**

- You've trained seriously for 1–3 years
- You can commit to 4 gym days a week (about 75 minutes each)
- You want visible muscle growth, not just strength numbers
- You have access to a full commercial gym (barbells, machines, cables)

**This program is NOT for you if:**

- You've trained less than a year → run a full-body 3-day program first; a
  little stimulus does a lot at your stage, and you'll grow faster on less
- You can only train 3 days a week → our 3-day advanced program (coming soon)
  fits better
- You're returning from injury → clear it with a professional first

**What you'll need:** a logbook (the included spreadsheet or printed sheets),
a way to time 2-minute rests, and honesty about how close each set is to failure.

## Page 3 — The promise, and how this program is different

Most programs hand you a table and say "trust me." This one doesn't.

Second Gear is built from 40+ sources — coaches like Jeff Nippard, Andy Baker,
J3 University, Westside Barbell, and the current hypertrophy research. Every
choice in these pages has a reason attached, and where the experts genuinely
disagree — how close to failure you should train, whether rear delts need
direct work, whether calves respond to gym work at all — we show you **both
positions** and tell you which one this program takes and why.

You'll also learn the skill that outlasts any program: reading your own
logbook. Twelve weeks from now, the numbers — not soreness, not motivation —
will tell you exactly what to do next. This program teaches you that loop.

**What to expect:** 4 sessions a week, two upper and two lower, every major
muscle trained twice weekly with 10–13 hard sets per week — the growth zone
for your training age. Progression runs in two blocks: Block 1 adds reps,
Block 2 adds sets. Deloads at weeks 5 and 12. Simple levers, applied
relentlessly.

## Page 4 — How to read this program

**RIR (Reps In Reserve):** how many reps you could still do before failure.
RIR 2 means you stopped 2 reps short. RIR 0 = you could not do another rep.

**Hard (working) sets:** the sets that count. Warm-up sets never count — do
1–2 light, easy ramp-up sets before the first exercise for each muscle, then
the numbered sets begin.

**Rest:** standardized at 2 minutes everywhere. Not because 2:00 is magic —
research supports 2–3 minutes for growth — but because you have ~19–23 hard
sets to fit into 75 minutes, and a clock you actually follow beats a perfect
rest you don't.

**Supersets:** marked pairs (A1/A2) performed back-to-back with one shared
2-minute rest. The pairs are chosen so the two exercises don't compete —
opposite muscles or different joints — so performance holds up.

**Effort by exercise type** (this matters):

- Heavy barbell compounds (squat, bench, deadlift, RDL): stop at **RIR 1–3**,
  even on the last set. Grinding these to failure costs recovery you'll pay
  for tomorrow, for little extra growth.
- Machines and cables: the **last set can go to failure**; earlier sets RIR 1–2.
- Isolations (raises, curls, pushdowns): **failure is fine regularly** — the
  fatigue cost is local and small.

## Page 5 — The weekly split

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Upper A (chest-led) |
| Tue | Lower A (quad-led) |
| Wed | Rest |
| Thu | Upper B (back-led) |
| Fri | Lower B (hinge-led — deadlift day) |
| Sat | Rest |
| Sun | Rest |

**Why this layout:**

- **Never more than 2 consecutive training days** — the Tues/Fri spacing keeps
  systemic fatigue from stacking up before each session.
- **Deadlifts on Friday**, in front of the weekend's two rest days — the most
  fatiguing lift gets the most recovery afterward.
- **Upper days alternate chest-led / back-led** so each pulling and pushing
  muscle gets a fresh start once a week.
- **Every muscle lands twice a week** — the consensus frequency for growth
  among nearly all our sources.

Life happens on non-negotiable weeks? See "Missed Sessions" (page 26).

## Pages 6–9 — The four sessions

### Upper A (Mon) — chest-led

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RIR | Rest |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|------|
| 1 | Barbell Bench Press | 3 × 6–10 | 1–3 | 2 min |
| 2 | Chest-Supported Row | 3 × 8–12 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 3 | Incline DB Press | 3 × 8–12 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 4 | Lat Pulldown | 3 × 8–12 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 5 | DB Lateral Raise | 3 × 12–15 | 0–1 | 2 min |
| 6 | **Superset** A1 Rope Pushdown · A2 Hammer Curl | 3 × 10–15 each | 0–1 | 2 min shared |

Coaching notes: bench first while fresh — it's the session's one heavy
free-weight compound and it stays 1–3 RIR. Incline press with a deep stretch
at the bottom; the lengthened position is where growth is stimulated. Full
stretch at the top of every pulldown. Side delts get direct work because
pressing alone doesn't build them.

Substitutions: bench → machine chest press (joint-friendly) · chest-supported
row → machine row or single-arm DB row · incline DB → incline Smith/machine
press · pulldown → pull-up.

### Lower A (Tue) — quad-led

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RIR | Rest |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|------|
| 1 | Back Squat | 4 × 6–10 | 1–3 | 2 min |
| 2 | Romanian Deadlift | 3 × 8–10 | 1–3 | 2 min |
| 3 | Seated Leg Curl | 3 × 10–15 | 0–1 | 2 min |
| 4 | Standing Calf Raise | 3 × 10–15 | 0–1 | 2 min |
| 5 | Cable Crunch | 3 × 10–15 | 0–1 | 2 min |

Coaching notes: full-depth squats — depth is the stimulus, not a bonus.
Seated leg curls over lying: the seated position trains the hamstring in its
lengthened state. Pause in the stretched position at the bottom of every calf
raise.

Substitutions: back squat → hack squat or Smith squat · RDL stays (it's the
hinge slot) · cable crunch → roman-chair raises (no equipment crunch needed).

### Upper B (Thu) — back-led

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RIR | Rest |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|------|
| 1 | Pull-Up (weighted when ready) | 3 × 6–10 | 1–3 | 2 min |
| 2 | Machine Incline Press | 3 × 8–12 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 3 | Seated Cable Row | 3 × 8–12 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 4 | Pec Deck / Cable Crossover | 2 × 10–15 | 0–1 | 2 min |
| 5 | **Superset** A1 Cable Lateral Raise · A2 Rear-Delt Fly | 3 × 12–15 each | 0–1 | 2 min shared |
| 6 | **Superset** A1 Overhead Triceps Extension · A2 Incline Curl | 3 × 10–15 each | 0–1 | 2 min shared |

Coaching notes: deep stretch at the bottom of every pull-up. The machine
press slots in second because machines deliver tension with less fatigue once
you're already worked. The overhead triceps extension is deliberate — the
overhead angle is what loads the long head, the biggest part of the triceps.

Substitutions: pull-up → lat pulldown · pec deck → cable crossover · any
curl/triceps pair keeps its overhead/stretch emphasis.

### Lower B (Fri) — hinge-led

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RIR | Rest |
|---|----------|-------------|-----|------|
| 1 | Conventional Deadlift | 3 × 6–8 | 1–3 | 2 min |
| 2 | Leg Press | 3 × 10–12 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 3 | Lying Leg Curl | 3 × 10–15 | 0–1 | 2 min |
| 4 | Hip Thrust | 3 × 8–12 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 5 | Seated Calf Raise | 3 × 10–15 | 0–1 | 2 min |

Coaching notes: deadlifts before the weekend (see page 5). Feet low and
narrow on the leg press — that's the quad stimulus position. Friday's calf
raise is seated on purpose: a different angle from Tuesday's standing raises.
Trap-bar deadlift is a fully equal substitution.

## Pages 10–11 — Why this volume (the chapter other programs don't have)

Every major muscle gets **10–13 hard sets per week**, spread across two
sessions with no more than ~7 sets of any muscle in one session. Here's where
that comes from, and where our sources disagree.

**The consensus:** the growth zone for most muscles is 10–20 hard sets per
week. Below ~6–8 you grow slowly; above ~20, returns shrink fast for most
people. Within a single session, about 6 hard sets per muscle is where added
sets stop adding much — which is why nothing here is smashed into one day.

**The disagreement (and our position):** one research-oriented camp argues
that above 10–12 weekly sets the return is minuscule for most lifters; the
coaching camp keeps clients growing at 15–20+. We take the **mid-band**
(10–13) because you're training for growth with good recovery and a full
four-day schedule — enough to grow briskly, not so much that your logbook
drowns in fatigue.

**Arms get "only" 6 direct sets each — on purpose.** Direct biceps/triceps
set targets of 3–6 hold for non-priority arms, because every one of your 12
back sets and 11 chest sets piles indirect arm work on top. Counting only the
curls and pushdowns as "arm volume" misunderstands how volume adds up.

**Where experts genuinely disagree — shown, not hidden:**

- **Rear delts.** Camp 1: rear delts are overrated — proper back training
  (12 sets/week here) covers them. Camp 2: they're everyone's lagging head
  and need priority work. **Our position:** Camp 1, with 3 direct sets as
  insurance. If your rear delts visibly lag on this program, they're the
  first place to add sets in Block 2.
- **Calves.** Camp 1: dedicated sets plus long stretch pauses work. Camp 2:
  calves respond to the load of daily life, and gym work mostly maintains
  them. **Our position:** a lean 6 sets with stretch pauses — if calves are
  your priority, this is the volume you'd increase first, and you'd be
  betting on Camp 1.
- **Failure training.** Camp 1 (research-leaning): never grind compounds,
  isolations can fail. Camp 2 (one strong coaching voice): RIR targets are
  imprecise crutches; all-out sets build the most. **Our position:** Camp 1's
  split by exercise type (page 4). If your logbook shows you grow on more
  all-out effort, that's a valid individual override — the numbers decide.

## Pages 12–13 — Progression: the engine of the program

Programs don't grow you; **progression** does. Second Gear runs two blocks,
each with one lever. Changing two things at once means you can't tell what
worked.

**Week 1 — calibration.** Choose loads that land mid-rep-range at **RIR 3**.
Deliberately conservative: you're buying room to climb. Log every set.

**Block 1 (weeks 2–4) — add REPS.** Same loads as week 1; add 1–2 reps per
set each week. When all sets of an exercise hit the top of its rep range,
add load next session (small jump) and restart at the bottom of the range.
This is double progression — the reps-first, load-second loop that runs the
whole program.

- *Worked example:* Week 1: bench 185 lb × 8, 8, 8 @ RIR 3. Week 2: 185 × 9,
  9, 9. Week 3: 185 × 10, 10, 9. Week 4: 185 × 10, 10, 10 → next session
  195 × 8, 8, 8.

**Week 5 — deload** (page 14).

**Block 2 (weeks 6–11) — add SETS.** Something must change after every block,
so the lever switches from reps to sets: each week, add 1 set to one upper
and one lower exercise — the ones still progressing. Cap 5 sets per exercise;
at the cap, add to a different exercise instead. Reps continue double
progression from week-5 levels.

- *Worked example:* Week 6: squat stays 4 × 6–10; leg press 3 × 10–12. Week
  7: leg press 4 × 10–12. Week 8 (post-deload): pulldown 3 → 4 sets.

**Week 12 — deload + re-test** (below).

## Page 14 — Deloads

Weeks 5 and 12: **halve every exercise's sets, keep the loads.** No new
exercises, no intensity techniques. It should feel easy — that means you
timed it right. (If your light week still feels hard, that's confirmation
you needed it.)

Why halve sets and not load? Volume is this program's stressor; cutting the
actual stressor recovers you, while dropping load just trains you lighter.

**Symptom override — deloads don't wait for the calendar.** If you're beat
up, soreness is lingering between sessions, warm-up weights feel heavy, or
the pump has vanished, take the deload early regardless of what week it is.

**Week 12 re-test:** after the final deload, repeat your week-6 loads on the
four main lifts and compare logged reps. That comparison is the program's
report card — and your evidence-based starting point for whatever's next.

## Page 15 — Autoregulation: reading the logbook

Run these checks after every session:

- **Reps flat or down for 2 straight sessions on an exercise** → the prior
  volume was too high: drop 1 set from that exercise and rerun.
- **Reps moving** → change nothing. If you're progressing, it's the right
  amount of work. The most common intermediate mistake is changing a working
  program.
- **Stalled a full block** → use Block 2's set-addition lever on the stalled
  lift, or swap the exercise (any stall lasting multiple blocks, or reps
  falling below ~6 at your loads, is a swap signal).
- **Joint pain on any movement** → substitute the listed alternative that
  session. Pain is the one filter that outranks every rule in this book.

The principle underneath all four: **the logbook is the judge.** Not
soreness, not the pump, not motivation.

## Page 16 — Missed sessions

Life happens. Rules for keeping the week intact:

- **Missed one session:** don't double up — never stack two sessions in one
  day. Drop the missed session and resume the schedule; at 2×/week
  frequency, every muscle still got one stimulus, and single-session
  frequency dips don't measurably stall growth.
- **Missed two:** convert to a full-body day — first exercise from each of
  the four sessions, 2 sets each, done in ~45 minutes. Better than nothing
  is an understatement; it's most of the stimulus.
- **A week off entirely** (travel, illness): resume where you left off, but
  repeat the previous week's loads at RIR 3 for the first sessions back.
  Don't punish yourself with a make-up week.

## Page 17 — After week 12

Three honest paths:

1. **Rerun Block 2** with your re-test numbers if the program was still
   progressing — the simplest win.
2. **Move to a new container** — a 3-day upper/lower/full program for busier
   seasons (our advanced 3-day program, built on the same principles, ships
   soon).
3. **Specialize** — if the re-test and the mirror agree one area lagged,
   that's when a specialization block earns its complexity. Not before.

Whatever you choose: carry the logbook habit with you. It outlasts every
program, including this one.

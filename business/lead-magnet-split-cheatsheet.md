## Pick your split in 60 seconds (first YES wins)

1. **Training less than ~1 year?** → **Full Body, 3×/week.** A little stimulus
   does a lot right now. No deloads needed yet.
2. **Sessions regularly run past ~1.5 hours, or late-session lifts stalling?**
   → **Move up the ladder:** Full Body → Upper/Lower → PPL.
3. **Fresh at the end of every session, fully recovered between?** → You can
   *drop* a day and concentrate quality — regression can be progress.
4. **Only ~45 minutes per session?** → **Body-part split** (max 2 muscle
   groups/day).
5. **Schedule won't commit to fixed weekdays?** → **Rotating PPL** (3 on, 1 off).
6. **Arms are the mission?** → **Torso/Limb, 6 days/week** — arms 4×/wk, no
   dedicated arm day.
7. **One muscle badly lagging (and you're not a beginner)?** →
   **Specialization:** cut a strong area to maintenance, reallocate that
   volume to the lagging one 2–3×/week. Volume is moved, never conjured.
8. **None of the above — healthy, consistent, 3–4 fixed days?** →
   **Upper/Lower.** The intermediate default and the sweet spot for most.

> Already progressing on every lift? **Change nothing.** That's the right program.

## How many sets per muscle per week?

| Goal | Hard sets/muscle/week |
|---|---|
| Just keep muscle | ~3 |
| Slow, minimal growth | 6–8 |
| **Main growth zone (default)** | **10–20** |
| High-volume responder (trial only) | up to 20–30 |

Guardrails: ~6 hard sets per muscle per *session* is the plateau; ≤5 sets per
exercise before switching angles; if next session's numbers drop, the previous
volume was too high — cut and rerun.

## How hard should each set be?

- **Heavy barbell compounds** (squat, bench, deadlift): stop 1–3 reps short of
  failure, even on the last set.
- **Machines/cables:** last set to failure is fine.
- **Isolations:** failure is fine regularly.
- Beginners: don't overthink effort — you can't push hard enough for it to
  matter yet. Log everything; the logbook is the judge.

## The 5 rules every expert agrees on

1. Volume dictates the split — never pick a split first and force volume in.
2. Consistency beats optimization — the best program is the one you'll run.
3. The logbook is the judge — not soreness, pump, or motivation.
4. Templates fit ~95% of people; fully custom design is for advanced athletes.
5. Specialize only after the foundation — "weak arms" at 3 months means weak
   everything.
